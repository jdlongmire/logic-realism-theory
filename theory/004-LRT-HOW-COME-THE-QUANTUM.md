# How Come the Quantum?

## Answers from the Triadic Reality Model and Logic Realism Theory

James D. Longmire
Northrop Grumman Fellow (unaffiliated research)
ORCID: 0009-0009-1383-7698

---

## 1. Wheeler's Question

In 1990, John Archibald Wheeler posed three questions that he believed captured the deepest puzzles of physics:

1. How come existence?
2. How come the quantum?
3. How come "one world" from among the quantum possibilities?

The first and third have generated enormous philosophical literature. The second has been surprisingly neglected — not because no one has tried to answer it, but because the dominant programs in quantum foundations have quietly changed the subject.

*Interpretation programs* (Copenhagen, Many-Worlds, Bohmian mechanics, GRW, relational quantum mechanics) explain what the quantum formalism means. They tell us whether the wavefunction is real, whether outcomes are unique, whether particles have definite trajectories. These are important questions. But they presuppose the formalism and ask about its referent. They do not explain why physical reality should be describable in those terms at all.

*Reconstruction programs* (Hardy 2001, Chiribella-D'Ariano-Perinotti 2011, Masanes and Müller 2011) take a different approach. They derive the quantum formalism from sets of operational axioms — reasonable-sounding constraints on how physical systems compose, transform, and yield information. This is genuine progress. If you accept the axioms, the Hilbert-space structure of quantum mechanics follows.

But the axioms themselves are left unexplained. Hardy's five "reasonable axioms," Chiribella *et al.*'s six informational principles, Masanes and Müller's five physical requirements — each set is internally consistent, each yields quantum theory, and none comes with an explanation of why a physical world should satisfy axioms of that kind rather than some other kind. The reconstruction answers "how come the quantum formalism given these axioms?" It does not answer "how come these axioms?"

Wheeler's question is prior to both programs. It asks not what the quantum means, nor how the quantum formalism can be derived, but *why there is anything quantum in the first place*.

This paper argues that the Triadic Reality Model (TRM) and Logic Realism Theory (LRT) supply an answer.


## 2. The Order of Explanation

The standard order of explanation in quantum foundations runs like this:

$$\text{Formalism} \longrightarrow \text{Interpretation or Axioms}$$

Start with the Hilbert space, the Born rule, the Schrödinger equation. Then either interpret these mathematical objects (what do they refer to?) or derive them from operational principles (what assumptions generate them?).

TRM-LRT reverses the order:

$$\text{Ontological conditions} \longrightarrow \text{Structural constraints} \longrightarrow \text{Quantum formalism}$$

The question is not "given quantum mechanics, what does it mean?" but "given what reality must be, what formalism must describe it?"

This reversal is not merely rhetorical. It changes what counts as an explanation. In the standard order, the quantum is a starting point — a brute fact about the world that we then try to make sense of. In the reversed order, the quantum is a *consequence* — something that follows from more primitive conditions on what it takes for anything to be actual at all.

The claim is strong but precise: if you accept that physical reality must satisfy certain minimal ontological conditions, then quantum structure is no longer a surprise. It is expected.


## 3. Three Primitives

### 3.1 The Observation

Every physical theory, regardless of its domain or interpretation, exhibits three features simultaneously:

1. **Logical structure.** The theory's predictions are self-consistent. Outcomes satisfy identity (a measurement result is what it is), non-contradiction (a photon is not simultaneously detected and not detected at the same location), and excluded middle (for any actualized sharp outcome, either the outcome obtained or it did not). No successful physical theory permits actualized measurement records that violate these conditions.

2. **Informational structure.** The theory posits a space of possible configurations — states that a system could occupy. Classical mechanics has phase space. Quantum mechanics has Hilbert space. Thermodynamics has macroscopic state space. The specific space varies; the existence of a configurational domain does not.

3. **Dynamical structure.** Something happens. Configurations change, measurements yield outcomes, systems evolve. No physical theory consists solely of a state space and a logic. There is always a principle that selects which possibilities are realized.

These three features are not artifacts of how we write theories down. They are conditions on what it takes for a physical theory to describe anything at all. A "theory" with no logical structure makes no definite predictions. A "theory" with no configurational space has nothing to be true about. A "theory" with no dynamical principle describes a frozen catalog, not a world.

### 3.2 The Thesis

The Triadic Reality Model takes these observations seriously as ontological claims. It proposes that physical reality is constituted by three irreducible co-primitives:

- **L₃** — the three classical laws of logic (Identity, Non-Contradiction, Excluded Middle) understood not as rules of thought but as ontological constraints on what can obtain.
- **I∞** — an informational domain: the complete space of logically admissible configurations. Not a physical container, but a structured totality of ways things could be arranged.
- **A** — the actualization principle: a primitive that selects which configurations from $I_\infty$ actually obtain. Not reducible to a law of physics, because laws of physics presuppose it.

Together these form the primitive ontology:

$$\chi \equiv [L_3 : I_\infty : A]$$

The colon notation signals co-constitution: each primitive requires the others to do its work. $L_3$ without $I_\infty$ constrains nothing (there must be configurations for logic to apply to). $I_\infty$ without $L_3$ has no structure (configurations without identity conditions are not configurations). $A$ without either has nothing to select and no criterion for selection.

### 3.3 Irreducibility

The primitives are irreducible in the sense that no two of them can absorb the third.

Consider the six possible reductions:

| Attempted reduction | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| $L_3$ absorbs $I_\infty$ | Logic without a domain of application constrains nothing; identity conditions require objects to be identical *to* |
| $L_3$ absorbs $A$ | Logical constraints restrict what can obtain but do not select what does; restriction is not actualization |
| $I_\infty$ absorbs $L_3$ | A domain of configurations without identity conditions is incoherent; "configuration" presupposes distinguishability |
| $I_\infty$ absorbs $A$ | A space of possibilities without a principle of actualization describes nothing actual; possibility is not actuality |
| $A$ absorbs $L_3$ | Actualization without logical constraint could select contradictory states; the result would not be a determinate world |
| $A$ absorbs $I_\infty$ | Actualization without a domain of possibilities has nothing to select; selection presupposes alternatives |

Each failure follows from the constitutive roles of the primitives. This is not a proof that no reduction could ever succeed — it is an argument that the most natural attempted reductions fail for identifiable reasons.

### 3.4 The Bridge

If $\chi$ constitutes physical reality, then it grounds an actualized domain $A_\Omega$ — the totality of what actually obtains. The relationship between the primitives and the actual world takes the form:

$$\chi \vdash A_\Omega$$

where $\vdash$ denotes constitutive grounding (not logical derivation). This says: the actual world exists *in virtue of* the primitive ontology.

The structure of $A_\Omega$ is then characterized by the bridge equation:

$$A_\Omega = L_3(I_\infty)$$

Read: the actualized domain is the set of all $L_3$-admissible configurations of the informational space. What obtains is what logic permits from the totality of possibilities.

This equation separates into three claims of decreasing strength:

1. **Grounding:** $\chi$ constitutes an actual domain $A_\Omega$. (Nearly uncontroversial: if the primitives are real, something actual results.)
2. **Characterization:** $A_\Omega \subseteq L_3(I_\infty)$. (Every actual configuration is logically admissible. Hard to deny without endorsing the actuality of contradictions.)
3. **Plenitude:** $A_\Omega = L_3(I_\infty)$. (Everything logically admissible is actual. The strongest claim; a downstream metaphysical extension that plays no role in the physics reconstruction.)

The physics reconstruction requires only claims 1 and 2. Plenitude is an optional further commitment — a metaphysical thesis about the exhaustiveness of actuality that may or may not hold. Nothing in the derivation chain from §4 onward depends on it. Readers who accept that actuality is logically constrained and informationally structured can engage the entire reconstruction without taking a position on plenitude.


## 4. From Ontology to Quantum Structure

This section sketches how the primitive ontology generates the structural features that reconstruction programs postulate. The full technical development appears in the companion paper (Longmire 2026b); the purpose here is to show that each step in the reconstruction is grounded rather than assumed.

### 4.1 Determinate Identity

If $L_3$ is ontologically constitutive, then every actualized configuration has a determinate identity. A thing is what it is ($A = A$), is not what it is not ($\neg(A \wedge \neg A)$), and for any well-formed predicate, either the predicate or its negation applies ($P \vee \neg P$).

This yields what we call the *Physical Proposition Criterion* (PPC): for any configuration in $A_\Omega$ and any physical proposition, there is a fact of the matter. This is not an assumption about measurement — it is a consequence of $L_3$ operating constitutively on the actual domain.

The PPC connects ontology to operational structure through a crucial bridge: $L_3$-satisfaction is not merely formal but requires *operational distinguishability*. Two configurations that satisfy identity conditions must be distinguishable in principle. Identity without the possibility of distinction is vacuous — a "difference" that makes no difference is not identity but indiscernibility.

### 4.2 Local Tomography

Reconstruction programs typically postulate that the state of a composite system is completely determined by the statistics of local measurements on its parts. This is *local tomography* — the principle that if you know everything about the parts, you know everything about the whole.

In TRM-LRT, local tomography follows from two conditions:

- **Metaphysical supervenience (H1):** Because $L_3$ enforces determinate identity on all actualized configurations, the global state of a composite system supervenes on the determinate identities of its components. There is no "extra fact" about the whole that is not grounded in facts about the parts.

- **Operational local tomography (H2):** Because composite configurations in $I_\infty$ are argued to be generated from the admissible configurations of their subsystems and relations, the informational domain supports local reconstruction. This product structure is not stipulated but follows from $L_3$-enforced identity conditions: if subsystems have independent determinate identities, then their joint possibility space is generated by their individual possibility spaces. A non-product structure would require "extra" joint configurations not traceable to the identities of the parts, violating the determinacy that $L_3$ enforces.

These two conditions are not postulated; they are consequences of how $L_3$ and $I_\infty$ operate. The first follows from the constitutive role of logical constraint. The second follows from the compositional structure of the informational domain, argued above.

### 4.3 Complex Hilbert Space

Given local tomography, a well-known result due to Masanes and Müller (2011) shows that the state space must be a complex Hilbert space. The key parameter is $K$, the number of real parameters needed to specify a state. Local tomography constrains $K$ to equal $d^2$ where $d$ is the dimension, and this selects the complex field uniquely among the possibilities (real, complex, quaternionic).

This result is imported, not re-derived. The contribution of TRM-LRT is not to replace the Masanes-Müller theorem but to explain why local tomography holds in the first place.

### 4.4 Projection-Valued Measures

The actualization principle $A$ operates as a binary selector: for any sharp event and any configuration, $A$ either actualizes it or does not. There is no middle ground — $L_3$ (specifically, excluded middle) forbids indeterminate actualization.

This binary character has a direct mathematical consequence. When sharp events are represented as operators on Hilbert space, their spectrum must be $\{0, 1\}$. An operator with spectrum $\{0, 1\}$ is an idempotent, and a self-adjoint idempotent is a projection. A complete family of sharp events therefore constitutes a projection-valued measure (PVM).

This derivation concerns *sharp* events — those for which $A$ delivers a determinate yes-or-no verdict. General measurements, including the positive operator-valued measures (POVMs) used in modern quantum information theory, are not derived here. They arise as coarse-grained or unsharp variants of the fundamental PVM structure, representing incomplete information about underlying sharp actualization events rather than a distinct ontological category.

This is where the ontology does real mathematical work. Standard reconstruction programs assume or stipulate that measurement is represented by PVMs. In TRM-LRT, PVM structure is *derived* from the binary character of actualization under $L_3$ constraint.

### 4.5 The Born Rule

Given PVM structure on a Hilbert space of dimension $d \geq 3$, Gleason's theorem (1957) shows that the only consistent probability assignment is the Born rule:

$$p(E \mid \psi) = \langle \psi \mid P_E \mid \psi \rangle$$

Gleason's theorem is imported. But notice what has happened: the premises of Gleason's theorem — a Hilbert space equipped with projection-valued measures — have been *derived* from the primitive ontology rather than assumed. The Born rule is not a sixth axiom added to the reconstruction; it is forced by the structure that the ontology generates.

### 4.6 Time and Dynamics

$\chi$ contains no temporal primitive. Time is not among the co-primitives. Instead, temporal structure emerges from the interaction of $A$ with $L_3$ and $I_\infty$:

1. $A$ produces a sequence of actualized configurations. Each actualization event yields a determinate state. Under the additional assumption that $L_3$-admissibility and the binary character of $A$ jointly exclude ambiguous succession, each state has a unique successor (the *Unique Next State* theorem, proven in the companion formalization; see Longmire 2026b, Step 7).

2. The sequence of actualized states is well-ordered. This gives ordinal time — discrete, directed, without gaps in the ordering.

3. Under additional continuity conditions (motivated by the topology of Hilbert space), the ordinal structure embeds in the real line, yielding continuous time.

4. The evolution operator — the map from one actualized state to the next — must preserve the Hilbert-space structure derived in §4.3. This means it is unitary.

5. Stone's theorem (1930) shows that every strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group on a Hilbert space has a self-adjoint generator $H$. The resulting evolution equation is the Schrödinger equation:

$$i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \lvert \psi(t) \rangle = H \lvert \psi(t) \rangle$$

The Planck constant $\hbar$ enters as an empirical scale factor connecting the mathematical generator to physical energy. It is not derived from the ontology — it is a fact about our world that fixes the units.


## 5. What This Answers

### 5.1 "How Come the Quantum?"

The answer is now available in a form that was not accessible to either interpretation or reconstruction programs alone:

Quantum mechanics has the form it does because physical reality is not primitively mathematical, probabilistic, or observer-defined. It is primitively logical, configurational, and actualized. The quantum appears as the formal reconstruction of what such a reality must look like under conditions of determinate identity, distinguishability, composition, and lawful succession.

More precisely:

- **Hilbert space** arises because the informational domain $I_\infty$ under $L_3$ constraint admits local tomographic reconstruction, and the only state space compatible with local tomography is complex Hilbert space (Masanes and Müller 2011).

- **Projection-valued measures** arise because the actualization principle $A$ operates as a binary selector under $L_3$ constraint, and the only operators with binary spectrum are projections.

- **The Born rule** arises because Gleason's theorem forces the unique probability measure on a Hilbert space equipped with PVMs.

- **Unitary evolution** arises because the sequence of actualized states must preserve the structure of the state space.

- **The Schrödinger equation** arises because Stone's theorem identifies the generator of any continuous unitary group as a self-adjoint operator.

At each step, the structural feature is not postulated but forced by the interaction of the primitives. The quantum is not a brute fact about formalism. It is a downstream consequence of what actuality requires.

### 5.2 What This Does Not Answer

The program does not derive quantum mechanics from nothing. It derives quantum structure from an explicit ontological commitment — one that must be defended on its own terms.

Several things remain outside the scope of the derivation:

- **Specific Hamiltonians.** The Schrödinger equation is reconstructed, but the particular Hamiltonian for any physical system depends on empirical input.
- **Planck's constant.** $\hbar$ enters as a dimensional scale factor. Why it has the value it does is not addressed.
- **Relativistic structure.** The reconstruction yields non-relativistic quantum mechanics. Extension to quantum field theory is an open problem.
- **The transcendental status of $I_\infty$.** While $L_3$ and $A$ are defended as transcendentally necessary, the ontological status of $I_\infty$ remains open (see §3.2). The physics reconstruction does not depend on resolving this question.

These are limitations, not evasions. The claim is that quantum structure is grounded in primitive ontological conditions — not that all of physics is derivable from pure reason.

### 5.3 The Contrast with Reconstruction Programs

Reconstruction programs and TRM-LRT are complementary, not competing. The relationship is best understood as follows:

**Reconstruction programs show:** Given suitable operational axioms (local tomography, continuous reversibility, information capacity constraints, purification, etc.), quantum theory follows.

**TRM-LRT adds:** Here is why a physically admissible world should satisfy axioms of that kind.

| Feature | Reconstruction programs | TRM-LRT |
|---|---|---|
| Local tomography | Postulated | Derived from $L_3$ + $I_\infty$ structure |
| PVM structure | Postulated or assumed | Derived from binary $A$ under $L_3$ |
| Complex field | Derived (given local tomography) | Derived (same route, grounded premise) |
| Born rule | Derived or implied | Derived (same route, grounded premises) |
| Schrödinger equation | Derived (given continuity) | Derived (same route, grounded time structure) |
| Why these axioms? | Left open | Answered: they follow from $\chi$ |

The reconstruction results are not replaced but *situated*. Hardy's axioms, Chiribella *et al.*'s principles, and Masanes-Müller's requirements are imported as established theorems. The original contribution is the ontological ground beneath them.


## 6. Objections and Responses

### 6.1 "This Is Just Renaming the Problem"

**Objection:** You have replaced "why the quantum?" with "why these three primitives?" — which is no easier to answer.

**Response:** The primitives are not arbitrary replacements. $L_3$ is defended as transcendentally necessary (its denial is performatively self-undermining: to argue against the law of non-contradiction, you must presuppose it). $A$ is defended as ontologically necessary via a distinct route (the denial of actualization is itself an actual claim). $I_\infty$ is defended at the level of pragmatic necessity and inference to best explanation, with its transcendental status explicitly left open.

The explanatory gain is real: quantum structure is shown to follow from conditions that are independently motivated, not from conditions chosen because they yield quantum theory. The reconstruction axioms are not brute facts about the world; they are consequences of more primitive facts about what it takes for anything to be actual.

### 6.2 "The Primitives Are Too Vague to Generate Physics"

**Objection:** $L_3$, $I_\infty$, and $A$ are philosophical categories, not mathematical axioms. The derivation smuggles in unstated assumptions.

**Response:** Every assumption in the derivation chain is stated and classified. The companion paper (Longmire 2026b) marks each step as ESTABLISHED (proven from stated premises or imported from published theorems), ARGUED (supported by explicit argument with identified assumptions), or OPEN (requiring further work). A Lean 4 formalization tracks the complete dependency graph, with zero unresolved proof obligations as of March 2026.

The derivation chain is not a black box. It is a sequence of explicitly justified steps from stated premises to stated conclusions, with every imported theorem cited and every gap labeled.

### 6.3 "Why Not Paraconsistent Logic?"

**Objection:** You assume classical logic ($L_3$). Paraconsistent and intuitionist alternatives exist. The choice is not innocent.

**Response:** $L_3$ is not adopted as a formal calculus among alternatives. It is adopted as a minimal set of identity conditions for determinate existence. A system that tolerates genuine ontological contradiction — not merely formal inconsistency as a modeling tool, but the actuality of a state that both obtains and does not obtain — collapses the conditions under which "obtaining" is meaningful. Paraconsistent *logics* are legitimate formal systems; paraconsistent *ontology* undermines the determinacy that makes physics possible.

### 6.4 "This Is Superdeterminism"

**Objection:** The plenitude claim $A_\Omega = L_3(I_\infty)$ sounds like every outcome is fixed in advance — superdeterminism in metaphysical dress.

**Response:** Superdeterminism claims that all events, including measurement choices, are predetermined by initial conditions, thereby denying statistical independence. As noted in §3.4, the physics reconstruction requires only the characterization claim ($A_\Omega \subseteq L_3(I_\infty)$), not plenitude. Under characterization alone, $A$ selects from within the logically admissible domain without external constraint on which admissible configurations are actualized. Nothing in the framework predetermines measurement settings or correlates them with hidden variables. Statistical independence is preserved; Bell-type experiments retain their force.

### 6.5 "This Is Just Structural Realism With Extra Steps"

**Objection:** Ontic structural realism (OSR) already says that physical structure is ontologically fundamental. What does TRM add?

**Response:** OSR identifies structure as fundamental but does not specify *which* structures are physically admissible or *why*. It provides a metaphysical orientation, not a derivation. TRM specifies the primitive ontology ($\chi$) and derives the structural constraints that physical theories must satisfy. The relationship is: OSR says structure is fundamental; TRM says *this* structure is fundamental and here is what follows from it.


## 7. The Explanatory Ambition

Reconstruction programs show how quantum theory follows from suitable axioms. TRM-LRT asks why a physically admissible world should satisfy axioms of that kind in the first place.

The program does not claim to have derived quantum mechanics from nothing, nor to have resolved every open question in the foundations of physics. It claims something more specific: that the structural features of quantum mechanics — Hilbert space, projection-valued measures, the Born rule, unitary evolution, the Schrödinger equation — are not brute facts about our world but downstream consequences of more primitive ontological conditions.

If this is correct, then Wheeler's question has an answer. The quantum is not an unexplained starting point. It is how a triadically structured reality — logical, informational, and actualized — becomes formally expressible.

That is "how come the quantum."
