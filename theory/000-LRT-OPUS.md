## Chapter Detail

### Ch 8: LRT Among Its Competitors

- Hardy (2001), CDP (2011), Masanes-Müller (2011): same destination, different starting points
- What LRT derives that others axiomatize: local tomography, PVM structure, temporal ordering
- Tegmark MUH: similar ambition, no actualization primitive
- Floridi ISR: shares information-first ontology, lacks L₃ + A
- Lewis modal realism: TAB is actualist
- Honest column: what LRT imports that others derive internally

### Ch 13: The Cost of Rejection

- Reject L₃: need alternative ground for determinate identity (dialetheism, conventionalism)
- Reject I∞: brute finite state space (why that cardinality?) or no information ontology
- Reject A: existence as brute fact or emergent from what?
- Reject bridge: operational axioms unsupported ("why these axioms?" unanswered)
- Structural observation: each rejection presupposes what it denies

### Ch 15: The Reasonable Paradigm Shift

**Core thesis:** LRT is conservative in every dimension except one: it insists "why?" is a legitimate question about physical axioms.

**What doesn't change:** The equations. The predictions (except cosmology gains a grounded w(z)). Laboratory practice. The formalism.

**What changes:** Brute postulates become grounded postulates. "Why complex Hilbert space?" answered. "Why the Born rule?" answered. "Why unitarity?" answered.

**Contrast with actual radical proposals:**

| Proposal | What it changes | Radical? |
|----------|----------------|----------|
| Many-Worlds | Ontology (infinite branching universes) | Yes |
| Bohmian mechanics | Dynamics (pilot wave + hidden variables) | Yes |
| QBism | Epistemology (QM is about agents, not reality) | Yes |
| **LRT** | **Foundations (why these axioms hold)** | **No** |

**Framing:** Lakatos progressive problem shift: same data, same formalism, but a research program that explains more with less ad hoc machinery.
