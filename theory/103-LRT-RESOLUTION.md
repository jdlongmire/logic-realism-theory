## Abstract

The word "collapse" encodes an ontological assumption: that the wavefunction is the primary bearer of physical reality, and that measurement interrupts its evolution through some dynamical mechanism yet to be identified. This paper argues that the assumption is a category error and that the measurement problem, as traditionally formulated, is malformed. Under Logic Realism Theory (LRT), where reality is constituted by three co-primitives — logical constraint ($L_3$), an informational domain of possible configurations ($I_\infty$), and a primitive actualization principle ($A$) — there is no wavefunction to collapse. Quantum states are descriptions of configurations in $I_\infty$; measurement outcomes are actualized configurations in $A_\Omega$. The transition from superposition to definite outcome is not a dynamical interruption of unitary evolution but the completion of logical resolution: decoherence provides the physical conditions under which $L_3$ becomes operative, $L_3$ (specifically Excluded Middle) supplies the logical verdict that a determinate outcome must obtain, and $A$ delivers that outcome. The measurement problem asked for a physical mechanism behind what is, at bottom, a logical requirement. Once the question is corrected, the answer is already present in the primitive ontology. This paper develops the Resolution Thesis in full, establishes the Zero-Gap identity (decoherence reaching operational completeness, $L_3$-evaluability, and $A$'s verdict are three descriptions of a single transition with no temporal separation), demonstrates its application to the standard measurement scenarios, and contrasts it with the dynamical collapse, decoherence-alone, and Everettian programs. Six lines of disagreement with Many-Worlds are developed: (1) $L_3$ is constitutive of reality, not perspectival per branch; (2) $A$ is Boolean — exactly one outcome obtains — making singular and universal actualization mutually exclusive; (3) LRT derives the Born rule via Gleason without the Everettian probability problem; (4) LRT is empirically falsifiable where MWI is empirically inert; (5) LRT's ontology is parsimonious where MWI posits uncountable branching; (6) LRT's dissolution of the measurement problem is more complete, generating no subsidiary problems. Finally, the paper establishes that LRT is falsifiable: the theory predicts that the entire GRW/CSL parameter space is empty, that no spontaneous collapse event occurs at any scale or rate, and that every null result in collapse-detection experiments is evidence for the Resolution Thesis over dynamical alternatives.

**Keywords:** quantum measurement, wavefunction collapse, category error, logical resolution, decoherence, actualization, quantum foundations, information ontology, logic realism

## 1. The Collapse Assumption

### 1.1 What "Collapse" Presupposes

The standard quantum measurement narrative runs as follows. A system is prepared in state $\lvert \psi \rangle = \sum_i c_i \lvert a_i \rangle$. The system evolves unitarily under the Schrödinger equation. Then measurement occurs, and the state "collapses" to some $\lvert a_k \rangle$ with probability $\lvert c_k \rvert^2$. This narrative is so embedded in the pedagogy of quantum mechanics that its ontological presuppositions are rarely examined. They are:

1. **Wavefunction primacy.** The quantum state $\lvert \psi \rangle$ is the ontologically primary description of the system — what the system *is*, not merely what we know about it.
2. **Dynamical completeness.** Unitary evolution is the only lawful dynamics. Any departure from unitarity requires a dynamical account — a mechanism, a modification of the Schrödinger equation, or at minimum a specification of when and where the departure occurs.
3. **Collapse as event.** The transition from superposition to definite outcome is a physical event that happens to the wavefunction. Something changes the state of reality, and we need to explain what.

These presuppositions generate the measurement problem. If the wavefunction is all there is, and if unitary evolution is the only dynamics, then no measurement outcome should ever occur — the system-apparatus composite should remain in a superposition of all outcomes. The fact that definite outcomes do occur requires either a modification of the dynamics (GRW/CSL), an expansion of the ontology (Many-Worlds), additional variables (Bohmian mechanics), or a retreat to instrumentalism (Copenhagen, QBism).

### 1.2 The Category Error

LRT's diagnosis is that all three presuppositions are false — not because the quantum formalism is wrong, but because the formalism is being asked to do ontological work it was never designed for.

The category error is this: *the measurement problem treats a logical requirement as if it were a dynamical deficit*.

The demand for a "collapse mechanism" presupposes that the transition from possibility to actuality is a physical process within the domain of possibility. But possibility and actuality are ontologically distinct domains. No process *within* $I_\infty$ can produce actuality, because $I_\infty$ is the domain of possible configurations and $A_\Omega$ is the domain of actual ones. The transition is not a dynamical event; it is the operation of the actualization primitive $A$ under logical constraint $L_3$.

Asking "what mechanism causes collapse?" is like asking "what force makes a contradiction false?" The answer is: no force. Contradictions are false in virtue of the law of Non-Contradiction, not in virtue of some dynamical process that eliminates them. Similarly, definite outcomes obtain in virtue of Excluded Middle and the Boolean character of $A$, not in virtue of some physical mechanism that interrupts unitary evolution.

### 1.3 The Corrected Question

If the collapse question is malformed, what is the well-formed question?

It is not: *How does the wavefunction collapse?*
It is: *Under what conditions does a degree of freedom enter the scope of actualization?*

This is the question Paper V posed. The present paper develops the positive account — the Resolution Thesis — that replaces the collapse narrative, and demonstrates (§3) that the threshold conditions are calculable from standard quantum mechanics rather than constituting a foundational gap.

## 2. The Resolution Thesis

### 2.1 Statement

**Resolution Thesis.** Measurement is not the collapse of a wavefunction but the completion of logical resolution. When a quantum degree of freedom enters physical conditions sufficient for $L_3$ to become operative with respect to that degree of freedom, Excluded Middle requires a determinate verdict, and $A$ delivers it. The process has three components:

1. **Decoherence provides the conditions.** Environmental interaction selects a preferred basis by suppressing off-diagonal terms in the density matrix, rendering the system's reduced state approximately diagonal in the pointer basis. This is a physical process within $I_\infty$, governed by unitary evolution of the system-environment composite.

2. **$L_3$ provides the verdict.** Once decoherence has rendered the alternatives mutually exclusive and exhaustive within the pointer basis — that is, once the system is in a configuration where the alternatives are $L_3$-admissible propositions — Excluded Middle requires that one alternative obtains and the others do not. Non-Contradiction requires that exactly one obtains. This is not a physical process but a logical constraint.

3. **$A$ delivers the outcome.** The actualization primitive selects one of the $L_3$-admissible alternatives. The selected outcome enters $A_\Omega$ as a determinate configuration. The selection is governed by the Born rule, which (per Paper II, Steps 5-6, via Gleason's theorem) is the unique probability measure on the projection-valued measure structure that $A$'s Boolean character forces.

Note that the measurement problem itself presupposes $L_3$. To ask "which outcome obtains?" requires that outcomes be determinate, distinguishable entities: Identity guarantees that each $\lvert a_i \rangle$ is what it is, Non-Contradiction guarantees that $a_i$ and $a_j$ ($i \neq j$) cannot co-obtain, and Excluded Middle guarantees that for each alternative, it either obtains or it does not. Without these constraints, the measurement problem cannot even be stated, because there are no determinate outcomes to select among. The question "why does one outcome obtain rather than another?" presupposes that outcomes are self-identical and mutually exclusive (TAB §2.4.0). This observation applies symmetrically to the competing programs: GRW posits spontaneous localization events that select one outcome from the superposition, but the very notion of "one outcome" selected from "many" requires Identity and Non-Contradiction to distinguish the alternatives and Excluded Middle to enforce that selection is exhaustive. Many-Worlds claims that all outcomes obtain on separate branches, yet the individuation of branches, and the assertion that each branch carries a determinate outcome, depends on $L_3$ operating within every branch. Both programs presuppose $L_3$ while treating it as a background condition rather than a constitutive principle. LRT alone makes $L_3$'s role explicit: it is not a silent prerequisite of the formalism but the ontological ground on which determinacy, and therefore the measurement problem, rests. Once $L_3$ is recognized as constitutive, the measurement problem does not require a solution; it dissolves, because the logical constraints that generated the question already contain the answer.

### 2.2 The Three Components Unpacked

#### Decoherence: Physical Conditions, Not Sufficient Cause

Decoherence is essential but incomplete. The decoherence program (Zurek 1981, 2003; Joos *et al.* 2003; Schlosshauer 2005, 2007) has demonstrated that environmental interaction:

- Selects a preferred (pointer) basis via einselection
- Suppresses interference terms on extraordinarily short timescales ($\sim 10^{-20}$ s for macroscopic objects)
- Produces an *improper mixture* — a reduced density matrix that is diagonal in the pointer basis

What decoherence does *not* do, and what its proponents have acknowledged (Zurek 2003; Schlosshauer 2005), is select one outcome from the mixture. The improper mixture represents the system's entanglement with the environment; it does not represent ignorance of a pre-existing definite outcome. The diagonal density matrix

$$\rho_S \approx \sum_i \lvert c_i \rvert^2 \lvert a_i \rangle \langle a_i \rvert$$

says that each outcome $a_i$ is weighted by $\lvert c_i \rvert^2$, but it does not say which one obtains. Decoherence explains the *absence of interference* between alternatives. It does not explain the *presence of a definite outcome*.

In LRT's vocabulary: decoherence is a process within $I_\infty$ that brings a configuration to the threshold of $L_3$-resolvability. It transforms a superposition (where the alternatives are not yet mutually exclusive propositions at the level of the pointer basis) into a decohered mixture (where they are). This is the physical work that decoherence does. It is necessary and substantial. But it is not the whole story.

#### $L_3$: The Logical Verdict

Once the alternatives are mutually exclusive and exhaustive — that is, once the configuration is such that the propositions "outcome $a_i$ obtains" and "outcome $a_j$ obtains" ($i \neq j$) are genuinely contradictory — $L_3$ becomes operative:

- **Identity:** Each alternative $a_i$ is what it is. The alternatives are determinate, distinguishable options.
- **Non-Contradiction:** It is not the case that both $a_i$ and $a_j$ ($i \neq j$) obtain. The system cannot be in two determinate states simultaneously.
- **Excluded Middle:** Either $a_i$ obtains or it does not. For the complete set of alternatives, exactly one must obtain.

This is not a physical mechanism. It is the application of the ontological constraints that constitute $L_3$ to a configuration that has been brought (by decoherence) into the scope where they apply. The "verdict" is the logical fact that a definite outcome is required.

#### $A$: Delivery

$A$ is the primitive that actualizes one alternative. It is Boolean:

$$A(a_i, c) \in \{1, 0\}$$

with exactly one $a_i$ receiving $A = 1$. The probability distribution over outcomes is the Born rule, forced by Gleason's theorem given the PVM structure that $A$'s Boolean character entails (Paper II, Steps 4-6).

$A$ is not a hidden variable. It does not carry information about which outcome will be selected prior to the conditions for selection being met. It is the irreducible operation by which one configuration among the $L_3$-admissible alternatives becomes actual. Its irreducibility is what makes the measurement problem *dissolve* rather than *be solved*: there is no further mechanism behind $A$ because $A$ is a primitive, and the demand for a mechanism behind it is the demand for a reduction that the ontology rejects.

### 2.3 Why This Is Not Just "Interpretation"

The Resolution Thesis differs from competing interpretations in a specific structural way: it does not add to the formalism, modify the formalism, or reinterpret the formalism. It *relocates the explanatory burden*.

| Program | What it does to the formalism | Status of "collapse" | Single definite outcome? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copenhagen | Adds a classical-quantum cut | Real but unexplained | Postulated |
| GRW/CSL | Modifies Schrödinger equation | Real dynamical process | Yes |
| Many-Worlds | Takes formalism literally | Absent (all branches real) | No — branch-relative only |
| Bohmian mechanics | Adds guidance equation + hidden variables | Apparent (particle always has definite position) | Yes |
| QBism | Reinterprets probabilities as credences | Credence update, not physical | Agent-relative |
| Decoherence-alone | No modification | Claimed to be explained; actually incomplete | Unexplained |
| **LRT Resolution** | **No modification** | **Category error: logical resolution, not dynamics** | **Yes ($A$ is Boolean)** |

The key distinction is between LRT and the decoherence-alone program. Both deny that collapse is a dynamical process. But the decoherence-alone program has no account of why one outcome rather than none obtains from the decohered mixture. LRT supplies this: $L_3$ requires it, $A$ does it.

## 3. The Zero-Gap Identity

### 3.1 Statement of the Identity

The Resolution Thesis (§2) presents three components — decoherence, $L_3$, $A$ — in expository sequence. But this sequence is pedagogical, not temporal. The formal claim is an identity:

$$H_{SE} \to \text{basis selection} + \text{coherence suppression} \;\equiv\; \text{events become } L_3\text{-evaluable} \;\equiv\; A \text{ delivers verdict}$$

The equivalence signs are the thesis. These are not three sequential steps with temporal gaps between them. They are three descriptions — physical, logical, ontological — of a single transition. Being decohered (in the sense that coherence is suppressed below operational detectability in the pointer basis) *is* being $L_3$-evaluable. Being $L_3$-evaluable *is* being subject to $A$'s Boolean verdict. There is no interval during which the system is decohered but not yet $L_3$-evaluable, and no interval during which it is $L_3$-evaluable but $A$ has not yet acted. The three descriptions pick out the same transition at different levels of analysis: $H_{SE}$ names the physical interaction, $L_3$-evaluability names the logical status, $A$'s verdict names the ontological completion.

This is the Zero-Gap claim: no temporal separation between decoherence reaching operational completeness and actualization occurring.

### 3.2 The Actualization Threshold as Calculational Task

The actualization threshold — the condition under which a degree of freedom enters the scope of $L_3$-resolution — is not an open foundational problem. It is a calculational task within standard quantum mechanics.

The relevant variables are already identified in the decoherence literature:

1. **The coupling Hamiltonian** $H_{SE}$, specifying the interaction between system and environment.
2. **The environmental spectral density** $J(\omega)$, characterizing the distribution of environmental modes that drive decoherence.
3. **The system Hilbert-space dimension** $d$, determining the structure of alternatives to be resolved.

Given these, the decoherence timescale $\tau_D$ and the suppression factor for off-diagonal coherence are calculable quantities (Zurek 2003; Schlosshauer 2007; Breuer and Petruccione 2002). For a macroscopic pointer interacting with a thermal environment, the coherence suppression factor scales as $\exp(-\Lambda t)$, where $\Lambda$ depends on $H_{SE}$ and $J(\omega)$ and is typically enormous — of order $10^{40}$ s$^{-1}$ for dust grains in thermal radiation (Joos *et al.* 2003).

The question "when does $L_3$ become operative?" thus reduces to: for what values of the interaction parameters is coherence suppressed to the point where the pointer-basis alternatives are operationally exclusive? This is a question about the physics of specific systems — coupling strengths, temperatures, particle numbers — not about the foundations of quantum mechanics.

### 3.3 No Free Parameters

This feature distinguishes LRT from dynamical collapse programs. GRW introduces two free parameters: a spontaneous collapse rate $\lambda_{\text{GRW}} \approx 10^{-16}$ s$^{-1}$ per particle and a localization width $r_C \approx 10^{-7}$ m (Ghirardi, Rimini, and Weber 1986). CSL adds a noise field with its own correlation structure (Pearle 1989). These parameters are tuned to reproduce macroscopic definiteness without disturbing microscopic coherence, but their values are not derived from anything — they are put in by hand.

LRT adds no free parameters. The conditions for resolution are fixed entirely by the system-environment interaction: the same Hamiltonian, spectral density, and Hilbert-space dimension that standard quantum mechanics already uses to calculate decoherence timescales. The theory inherits the decoherence program's quantitative predictions without supplementing them with adjustable constants.

### 3.4 Principled Physical Completeness and the Asymptotic Objection

A standard objection to decoherence-based accounts is that decoherence is asymptotic: off-diagonal terms are suppressed but never exactly eliminated. The density matrix is never *exactly* diagonal. Therefore — so the objection runs — the alternatives are never *exactly* mutually exclusive, $L_3$ never *exactly* applies, and resolution never *exactly* occurs.

LRT's response invokes Principled Physical Completeness (PPC): once coherence is suppressed below the threshold of operational detectability — below the precision of any physically realizable measurement — the residual coherence has no physical significance. The off-diagonal terms that persist at $10^{-10^{20}}$ are not imprecisions awaiting correction; they are artifacts of the mathematical formalism that outrun the physical ontology. Resolution is complete when coherence is operationally suppressed, not when it is mathematically zero.

PPC is not an approximation or a convenience. It is a principled claim about the relationship between mathematical description and physical reality: the mathematical limit $t \to \infty$ is not a precondition for physical definiteness. The decoherence timescale $\tau_D$ marks the onset of resolution, not an asymptotic approach to it.

### 3.5 The Critical Distinction: Resolution Is Not Decoherence

The Zero-Gap claim must not be misread as equating resolution with decoherence. Decoherence explains the loss of interference — why the off-diagonal terms vanish in the pointer basis. It does *not* explain the selection of an outcome. This distinction is precisely what separates LRT from the decoherence-alone program and from Everettian interpretations.

Everettians accept decoherence without selection: all branches persist, and the appearance of a single outcome is perspectival. The decoherence-alone program claims selection occurs but provides no mechanism for it. LRT provides $A$: the primitive that delivers the Boolean verdict — exactly one outcome obtains, the rest do not.

Zero-Gap means that decoherence reaching operational completeness and $A$'s verdict are temporally coincident. It does *not* mean they are the same operation. Decoherence is a physical process within $I_\infty$ — the unitary evolution of the system-environment composite that suppresses coherence. $A$'s verdict is the ontological transition from $I_\infty$ to $A_\Omega$ — the actualization of one $L_3$-admissible alternative. They are co-temporal but categorially distinct. The physical process provides the conditions; the ontological primitive provides the outcome.

The analogy: the mathematical derivation of a theorem and the logical necessity it establishes are temporally coincident (the theorem is necessary the moment it is derived) but categorially distinct (the derivation is an epistemic process; the necessity is a logical fact). Similarly, decoherence (physical process) and actualization (ontological fact) coincide without being identical.

### 3.6 Mathematical Resolution and Physical Actualization

There is a structural parallel between mathematical resolution and physical actualization that the Zero-Gap identity makes visible.

In mathematics, a system of equations with free variables becomes *resolved* when sufficient constraints are supplied to determine the values of those variables. A system with $n$ unknowns and $n$ independent constraints generically has a unique solution: the constraints *resolve* the indeterminacy. The resolution is driven by logical structure — the constraints, together with the laws of deduction, leave no room for alternatives.

Physical actualization has the same formal shape. A quantum system with multiple $L_3$-admissible alternatives becomes resolved when decoherence supplies sufficient physical constraint (basis selection, coherence suppression) for $L_3$ to operate. The logical structure — Identity, Non-Contradiction, Excluded Middle — then requires that exactly one alternative obtains. Both mathematical and physical resolution are driven by $L_3$ once sufficient constraint is supplied.

The disanalogy is equally important. Mathematical resolution is deterministic: given the constraints, the solution is unique. Physical resolution is probabilistically constrained: the Born rule fixes the probability of each outcome, but does not determine which outcome obtains. The residual indeterminacy — the fact that $A$ selects one outcome with probability $|c_k|^2$ rather than being forced to a unique solution — is where $A$'s primitive character lives. It is the irreducible contribution that actualization makes beyond what logical constraint alone can deliver.

This is not a deficiency. It is a structural feature of physical ontology as distinct from mathematical ontology. In mathematics, constraint is all there is: sufficient constraints yield a unique solution. In physics, constraint narrows the field ($L_3$ plus decoherence produce a finite set of admissible alternatives with definite weights), but the final selection requires a primitive — $A$ — that operates within the space constraint has defined. The Born rule is the measure on that space; $A$ is the selection within it.

### 3.7 Condition and Consequence: Why Zero-Gap Is Not Synonymy

The Zero-Gap thesis invites a misreading opposite to the one addressed in §3.5. Where that section warned against collapsing resolution into decoherence, the present concern is subtler: if the temporal gap between decoherence reaching operational completeness and actualization is zero, are the two not simply synonyms?

They are not. Decoherence is the condition; resolution is the consequence. These stand in a constitutive relation, not a relation of synonymy. Consider a parallel: the temperature at which water reaches 0°C and the onset of crystallization are temporally coincident, but "reaching the freezing point" and "crystallizing" are not synonyms. The first names the thermodynamic condition; the second names the structural consequence. The gap between them is zero. They remain categorially distinct.

The equivalence chain

$$H_{SE} \to \text{basis selection} + \text{coherence suppression} \;\equiv\; \text{events become } L_3\text{-evaluable} \;\equiv\; A \text{ delivers verdict}$$

is an identity of *extension*: the three descriptions pick out the same set of physical situations. It is not an identity of *intension*: they differ in what they say about those situations. The first description speaks the language of Hamiltonians and density matrices. The second speaks the language of logical admissibility. The third speaks the language of ontological completion. That all three apply simultaneously is the content of the Zero-Gap thesis. That they say different things is what prevents the thesis from collapsing into tautology.

The distinction matters for objection-handling. If Zero-Gap were synonymy, then "decoherence explains outcomes" would follow trivially, and $L_3$ and $A$ would be idle wheels. But decoherence (the physical process) does not explain outcomes; it provides the conditions under which the logical and ontological components become operative. The three descriptions are co-extensional without being co-intensional, and the non-trivial explanatory work of the Resolution Thesis lives in precisely this gap between extension and intension.

## 4. Applications

### 4.1 The Double-Slit Experiment

**Setup.** A particle passes through a double slit and is detected at a screen.

**Standard narrative.** Without which-path information, the particle "goes through both slits" as a superposition and interferes with itself. With which-path detection, the interference pattern disappears — the wavefunction "collapses" at the slit.

**Resolution account.** The particle's path degree of freedom is a configuration in $I_\infty$. Without which-path detection, the configuration remains unresolved with respect to path: the two-slit superposition evolves unitarily and produces interference at the screen. The interference pattern is a feature of the configuration's structure in $I_\infty$, not of a physical object passing through both slits.

When a which-path detector is introduced, it decoheres the path degree of freedom: the detector-particle interaction produces entanglement that suppresses interference between the two path alternatives. The path alternatives become $L_3$-resolvable — they are now mutually exclusive propositions about which slit the particle traverses. Excluded Middle requires a verdict. $A$ delivers one. The interference pattern disappears because the configuration is now resolved with respect to path, not because a wavefunction was interrupted.

The transition from "interference" to "no interference" is the transition from an unresolved configuration in $I_\infty$ to a resolved one — from a state where $L_3$ is not yet operative with respect to path to one where it is.

### 4.2 Schrödinger's Cat

**Standard narrative.** A cat in a sealed box is entangled with a radioactive atom. Until the box is opened, the cat is in a superposition of alive and dead. "Collapse" occurs upon observation.

**Resolution account.** The cat is a macroscopic system. Its degrees of freedom are decohered on timescales of order $10^{-20}$ seconds. The cat's viability (alive/dead) is resolved essentially instantaneously after the radioactive decay event — long before any observer opens the box.

What resolves the cat's state is not observation but the physical conditions of decoherence acting on the cat's macroscopic degrees of freedom. Once the decay has occurred and the lethal mechanism has been triggered, the cat's state is decohered into $L_3$-resolvable alternatives. Excluded Middle requires a verdict. $A$ delivers it. The cat is determinately alive or dead from the moment the physical conditions produce decoherence, regardless of whether anyone looks.

The "paradox" arises from treating the observer as necessary for collapse. Under the Resolution Thesis, the observer is irrelevant to resolution. What matters is decoherence — the physical conditions under which $L_3$ becomes operative. The cat is not in superposition, and never was, because the conditions for logical resolution are met almost instantaneously at macroscopic scales.

### 4.3 Wigner's Friend

**Setup.** Wigner's friend performs a measurement inside a sealed laboratory. Wigner, outside, describes the laboratory (friend included) as a quantum system in superposition.

**Standard narrative.** There is a tension between the friend's experience of a definite outcome and Wigner's description of the laboratory as being in superposition. Recent extended Wigner's friend scenarios (Frauchiger and Renner 2018; Brukner 2018) sharpen this into apparent contradictions.

**Resolution account.** The friend's measurement apparatus interacts with the quantum system within the sealed laboratory. This interaction produces decoherence of the measured observable relative to the apparatus pointer basis. Within the laboratory, the conditions for $L_3$-resolution are met: the alternatives become mutually exclusive and exhaustive. Excluded Middle requires a verdict. $A$ delivers it. The friend observes a definite outcome.

Wigner's description of the laboratory as "in superposition" is a description of the laboratory's configuration in $I_\infty$ relative to *Wigner's* accessible degrees of freedom. The information about which outcome the friend obtained is encoded in the laboratory-environment entanglement and is not available to Wigner without opening the laboratory. But the outcome is *resolved* — it belongs to $A_\Omega$ — regardless of Wigner's description. Wigner's superposition is not a statement about reality's indeterminacy; it is a statement about Wigner's informational access to a resolution that has already occurred.

The tension dissolves: there is no contradiction between "the friend has a definite outcome" and "Wigner describes the laboratory in superposition," because the first concerns $A_\Omega$ and the second concerns a configuration in $I_\infty$ relative to an external observer's informational perspective.

### 4.4 Delayed Choice and Wheeler's Smoky Dragon

**Setup.** In Wheeler's delayed-choice experiment, the decision to observe interference or which-path information is made after the particle has passed through the slits.

**Standard narrative.** The experimenter's later choice "retroactively determines" whether the particle went through one slit or both. The past seems to depend on the future.

**Resolution account.** No degree of freedom is resolved until the conditions for $L_3$-resolution are met. The path degree of freedom remains unresolved in $I_\infty$ as the particle traverses the slits — not because the particle is "in two places at once" but because no physical process has yet decohered the path alternatives into $L_3$-resolvable propositions. The decision to insert or remove the final beam splitter determines which degree of freedom is decohered at the detection stage: with the beam splitter, the which-path information is erased and interference is restored; without it, the path alternatives are decohered and resolved.

Nothing retroactive occurs. The configuration in $I_\infty$ evolves unitarily throughout. The timing of resolution depends on the timing of decoherence, which depends on the experimental arrangement. The "delayed choice" is delayed decoherence, and there is nothing mysterious about the conditions for resolution being established at a later time than the passage through the slits.

## 5. Why the Measurement Problem Was Malformed

### 5.1 Three Problems, Three Dissolutions

Paper V (§1) identified three sub-problems: the problem of outcomes, the preferred basis problem, and the problem of statistics. The Resolution Thesis addresses each:

**The problem of outcomes** asks: why does a definite outcome occur when unitary evolution predicts continued superposition? The Resolution Thesis answers: unitary evolution governs configurations in $I_\infty$. Definite outcomes belong to $A_\Omega$. The transition is not a failure of unitarity but the operation of $A$ under $L_3$ when decoherence provides the conditions. Unitarity is not violated because unitarity governs $I_\infty$ and resolution governs the $I_\infty \to A_\Omega$ interface.

**The preferred basis problem** asks: what determines which observable is measured? The Resolution Thesis answers: decoherence determines the pointer basis — the basis in which environmental interaction renders the alternatives $L_3$-resolvable. This is the einselection mechanism (Zurek 2003), adopted without modification. The preferred basis is not imposed by an observer or by an ad hoc postulate; it is selected by the physical dynamics of system-environment interaction.

**The problem of statistics** asks: why the Born rule? The Resolution Thesis inherits the answer from Papers II and IV: the Born rule is the unique probability measure on a Hilbert space equipped with projection-valued measures (Gleason 1957), and the PVM structure is forced by the Boolean character of $A$ under $L_3$. The statistics are not an additional postulate but a theorem given the ontological structure.

### 5.2 The Source of the Malformation

The measurement problem was malformed because it presupposed that all legitimate explanations must be dynamical. This presupposition is natural within a framework where the wavefunction is ontologically primary — if the wavefunction is all there is, then any change to the wavefunction must have a dynamical account.

But the wavefunction is not all there is. Under LRT, the wavefunction is a description of a configuration in $I_\infty$. The actualized domain $A_\Omega$ is ontologically distinct from $I_\infty$. The transition from $I_\infty$ to $A_\Omega$ is not a change within a single ontological domain; it is the interface between two domains.

Demanding a dynamical mechanism for this interface is like demanding a causal mechanism for the fact that contradictions don't obtain. The non-obtaining of contradictions is not caused by anything — it is constituted by $L_3$. Similarly, the obtaining of definite outcomes is not caused by a physical mechanism — it is constituted by $L_3$ acting through $A$ on configurations that decoherence has brought to the threshold of resolvability.

### 5.3 Decoherence Provides Conditions, $L_3$ Provides the Verdict, $A$ Delivers It

This three-part slogan captures the Resolution Thesis in a form that can be tested against every measurement scenario:

1. **Is there a decoherence mechanism?** Identify the physical interaction between system and environment that produces einselection and suppresses interference. If no such mechanism exists (e.g., in a perfectly isolated system), no resolution occurs and the configuration remains unresolved in $I_\infty$.

2. **Are the alternatives $L_3$-resolvable?** After decoherence, are the remaining alternatives mutually exclusive and exhaustive with respect to the pointer basis? If yes, $L_3$ requires a verdict.

3. **Does $A$ deliver a definite outcome?** Yes, by its Boolean character, with probability given by the Born rule.

The three-part structure explains why decoherence is necessary but not sufficient (it provides conditions but not the verdict), why $L_3$ is necessary but not independently operative (it provides the verdict but only when conditions are met), and why $A$ is necessary but not informative in isolation (it delivers the outcome but only when both conditions and verdict are in place).

## 6. Comparison with Competing Programs

### 6.1 Dynamical Collapse (GRW/CSL)

The GRW (Ghirardi, Rimini, and Weber 1986) and CSL (Pearle 1989; Ghirardi, Pearle, and Rimini 1990) programs modify the Schrödinger equation by adding stochastic terms that produce spontaneous localization. The modification introduces two free parameters: a collapse rate $\lambda_{\text{GRW}} \approx 10^{-16}$ s$^{-1}$ per particle and a localization width $r_C \approx 10^{-7}$ m.

**Agreement with LRT:** Both programs hold that definite outcomes are real, objective, and not observer-dependent. Both deny that unitary evolution alone is sufficient for definite outcomes.

**Disagreement:** GRW/CSL treats collapse as a genuine dynamical process — a physical event that modifies the state vector. LRT denies this. Under the Resolution Thesis, what GRW/CSL models as spontaneous collapse is better understood as the onset of decoherence-driven $L_3$-resolution. The GRW rate $\lambda$ parameterizes the *conditions* under which resolution occurs, but the resolution itself is logical, not dynamical.

**Empirical discrimination:** GRW/CSL makes specific predictions that differ from standard quantum mechanics at mesoscopic scales — anomalous heating of bulk matter, spontaneous radiation from charged particles, and deviations from unitarity in interferometric experiments (Adler 2007; Bassi *et al.* 2013). Current experiments constrain but have not yet confirmed or excluded the GRW parameters. LRT predicts no deviation from standard quantum mechanics at any scale: the formalism is not modified, only grounded differently. Detection of GRW-type anomalies would be evidence against the Resolution Thesis; their continued absence is consistent with it.

### 6.2 Decoherence-Alone

The decoherence program (Zurek 1981, 2003; Joos *et al.* 2003; Schlosshauer 2005, 2007) demonstrates that environmental interaction suppresses interference between pointer-basis alternatives on extremely short timescales. Some proponents have suggested that decoherence, by itself, solves the measurement problem.

**Agreement with LRT:** Decoherence is essential. The einselection mechanism correctly identifies the pointer basis. The timescales are correct.

**Disagreement:** Decoherence alone does not produce outcomes. The decohered density matrix

$$\rho_S \approx \sum_i \lvert c_i \rvert^2 \lvert a_i \rangle \langle a_i \rvert$$

is an *improper* mixture — it arises from tracing over the environment, not from ignorance of a pre-existing definite state. As Schlosshauer (2005) notes: "Decoherence tells us why we do not observe certain things (such as interference at the macroscopic level). It does not tell us why we observe particular things."

LRT supplies what decoherence lacks: the $L_3$-verdict that a definite outcome must obtain, and the $A$-primitive that delivers it. The Resolution Thesis can be understood as completing the decoherence program with the ontological resources it was missing.

### 6.3 Many-Worlds (Everett)

The Everettian program (Everett 1957; DeWitt 1970; Wallace 2012; Carroll 2019) takes the quantum formalism at face value: the wavefunction never collapses, all branches of the superposition are equally real, and the appearance of collapse is explained by decoherence-induced branching. The program is intellectually serious and enjoys increasing advocacy among physicists of realist sympathies. The disagreements with LRT are therefore correspondingly deep — not peripheral quibbles about interpretation but conflicts over the constitution of reality.

**Agreement with LRT:** Both deny that collapse is a dynamical process. Both take decoherence seriously as the mechanism that produces effective classicality. Both reject Copenhagen's classical cut and QBism's agent-relative probability.

**Six lines of disagreement follow.**

#### (i) $L_3$ as Ontological Constraint vs. Perspectival Appearance

LRT's central claim is that $L_3$ — Identity, Non-Contradiction, and Excluded Middle — is constitutive of reality, not merely of how observers carve reality up. Excluded Middle, in particular, is an ontic constraint: for any actualization-eligible degree of freedom, exactly one outcome obtains and the others do not. This is not a feature of the observer's perspective; it is a feature of $A_\Omega$ itself.

Many-Worlds relativizes Excluded Middle to branches. Within branch $b_k$, it is true that outcome $a_k$ obtained and not $a_j$ ($j \neq k$). But globally, all branches are equally real: $a_k$ obtains in $b_k$, $a_j$ obtains in $b_j$, and every $L_3$-admissible outcome obtains somewhere. Excluded Middle is thus reduced from an ontological constraint on what is actual to a perspectival feature of what an observer within a given branch records.

This is precisely the epistemic reading of $L_3$ that LRT rejects at its foundations. If Excluded Middle holds only relative to a branch, it is not governing reality — it is governing appearances. The distinction between actuality and possibility, which is the core of the $I_\infty / A_\Omega$ ontology, collapses: all possibilities are actualized, differing only in which branch they inhabit. $L_3$, in the Everettian scheme, does not constrain what is; it filters what a given observer sees. LRT and Many-Worlds are therefore not merely rival interpretations of quantum mechanics but rival positions on the fundamental question of whether logic is constitutive of reality or derivative of perspective.

#### (ii) The Mutual Exclusivity of Single-Outcome Reality

LRT holds that for any actualization event, $A$ selects exactly one outcome: $A(a_k, c) = 1$ for one $k$, zero for all others. The non-selected alternatives are configurations in $I_\infty$ — genuinely possible, never actual. Many-Worlds holds that all outcomes are actual, each in its own branch. These are not compatible interpretations of the same underlying structure; they are mutually exclusive claims about whether the actual world is one or many.

The incompatibility can be stated formally. Let $\{a_i\}$ be a complete set of $L_3$-admissible alternatives for a given actualization event. LRT asserts:

$$\exists!\, k : A(a_k, c) = 1, \quad A(a_j, c) = 0 \text{ for all } j \neq k$$

Many-Worlds asserts that for each $i$, there exists a branch $b_i$ in which $a_i$ is the obtained outcome. If branches are ontologically real, then every $a_i$ is actual — in some branch. The existential uniqueness claim of LRT is false in a branching ontology, because the uniqueness holds only branch-internally, not globally.

There is no reconciling these claims. One theory posits singular actualization; the other posits universal actualization with branch-relative experience. Choosing between them is not a matter of interpretive taste but of what one takes the modal distinction between possibility and actuality to mean.

#### (iii) The Probability Problem

In a deterministic branching universe, every outcome occurs with certainty in some branch. What, then, does it mean to say that outcome $a_k$ has probability $\lvert c_k \rvert^2$? The Born weights appear in the quantum formalism, but the Everettian ontology gives them no obvious role: all branches are equally real regardless of their amplitudes.

This is the Everettian probability problem, and it has resisted clean resolution despite substantial effort. The main proposals are:

- **Decision-theoretic:** Deutsch (1999) and Wallace (2012) argue that a rational agent who is certain that all branches will obtain should nonetheless act as if the Born probabilities are the probabilities of experiencing each branch. This approach relies on significant auxiliary assumptions about rational preference under branching and is contested (Kent 2010; Baker 2007).
- **Self-locating uncertainty:** Vaidman (2012) argues that before observing the outcome, an agent in a branching world is uncertain about which branch they will find themselves in, and that the Born weights measure this self-locating probability. Critics note that the framework requires treating branch weights as a measure of "how much of you" is in each branch, an assumption that is not obvious from the formalism.
- **Branch counting:** Naively, if each branch is equally real, each should receive equal probability — but this gives probability $1/n$ for $n$ branches, not the Born rule. Recovering the Born rule by branch counting requires additional structure.

LRT faces no probability problem. The Born rule is derived, not postulated: it is the unique probability measure on the projection-valued measure structure forced by $A$'s Boolean character, as established by Gleason's theorem (Gleason 1957; Paper II, Steps 5–6). Since $A$ selects exactly one outcome, probability is a measure of how likely each outcome is to be the one selected — a perfectly standard probabilistic concept. There is no tension between the Born weights and the ontology.

#### (iv) Empirical Inertness vs. Falsifiability

Many-Worlds makes no empirical prediction that differs from standard quantum mechanics. It takes the formalism literally, adds the ontology of branching, and claims that this dissolves the measurement problem without generating new testable content. No experiment can distinguish a Many-Worlds universe from a single-world universe with collapse — or from a single-world universe with LRT resolution — because all three agree on every observable distribution over outcomes.

LRT, by contrast, makes a novel empirical prediction: the GRW/CSL parameter space is empty ($\lambda = 0$, $r_C$ nonexistent). This prediction is testable and currently favored by experiment (§8). The Everettian program cannot make this claim because it makes no predictions at all beyond the formalism it interprets.

This asymmetry represents a significant methodological advantage for LRT. Many-Worlds resolves the measurement problem by accepting all outcomes and explaining away apparent singularity. LRT resolves it by providing a principled account of why exactly one outcome obtains, which account entails a testable prediction about the absence of dynamical collapse. The former is unfalsifiable; the latter has already been partially confirmed by experiment.

A theory that is immune to falsification is not on that account refuted, but it is methodologically subordinate to an alternative that makes the same observational predictions and additionally generates novel testable content. LRT provides that alternative.

#### (v) Ontological Parsimony

Many-Worlds posits that every quantum interaction ramifies reality into new branches. For a universe with $10^{80}$ particles undergoing constant environmental interaction, the branching rate is astronomical. The Everettian ontology is maximally profligate: every possible outcome of every interaction is actual, in some branch. The total reality is the universal wavefunction $\lvert \Psi \rangle$ — a structure of unimaginably vast and continuously ramifying complexity.

LRT's ontology is comparatively parsimonious. There is one actual world: $A_\Omega$. It is governed by $L_3$ and constituted by configurations that $A$ has actualized from the domain $I_\infty$ of possible configurations. The branching structure of Many-Worlds is entirely absent. In LRT's terms, the non-actualized alternatives are configurations in $I_\infty$ — genuine possibilities, logically tractable, but not actual. They do not proliferate into a multiverse; they simply remain possible.

The ontological economy favors LRT. Parsimony is not by itself a decisive criterion — a theory with greater ontological commitments may be correct — but when two theories make identical observational predictions, parsimony is a legitimate discriminating consideration. Many-Worlds posits uncountable branching; LRT posits a single actual world governed by three co-primitives. LRT achieves the same observational coverage with significantly less ontological machinery.

#### (vi) Quality of Dissolution

Both LRT and Many-Worlds claim to *dissolve* the measurement problem rather than solve it. The dissolution strategies differ in completeness.

Many-Worlds dissolves the problem of why collapse occurs by denying that collapse occurs — all branches persist, so there is nothing to collapse. But this generates new problems: the probability problem (§iii above), the preferred basis problem (what determines the branches?), and the question of what makes branches "real" in the relevant sense. Decoherence addresses the preferred basis question by providing einselection, but it does not deliver unique outcomes; it delivers decohered branches, each of which is equally real.

LRT dissolves the measurement problem more completely. It denies collapse (like Many-Worlds) but also maintains single outcomes (unlike Many-Worlds). The preferred basis is provided by einselection (shared with Many-Worlds). The probability problem does not arise because the Born rule is derived, not assumed. No new ontological structure (branches, worlds) is posited; the existing structure ($L_3$, $I_\infty$, $A$) is sufficient.

The dissolution taxonomy is therefore:

- **GRW/CSL:** Solves by modification — adds stochastic dynamics to explain collapse.
- **Many-Worlds:** Dissolves by expansion — denies singularity, posits branching, generates subsidiary problems.
- **LRT:** Dissolves by correction — identifies the category error, provides logical resolution, generates no subsidiary problems.

The corrective dissolution is methodologically superior to the expansive dissolution: it preserves the commitment to single outcomes that motivates the problem in the first place, while eliminating the dynamical mechanism demand that generated the pseudoproblem.

### 6.4 Bohmian Mechanics

Bohmian mechanics (Bohm 1952; Dürr, Goldstein, and Zanghì 1992) adds definite particle positions and a guidance equation to the quantum formalism. Particles always have definite positions; the wavefunction guides their motion.

**Agreement with LRT:** Both insist that definite outcomes really occur. Both deny that measurement outcomes are observer-dependent.

**Disagreement:** Bohmian mechanics locates definiteness in hidden variables (particle positions) that exist at all times. LRT locates definiteness in the actualization primitive $A$ operating under $L_3$, with resolution occurring when decoherence provides the conditions. Bohmian mechanics requires a preferred basis (position) as primitive; LRT derives the preferred basis from einselection.

Bohmian mechanics is also explicitly nonlocal: the guidance equation couples all particles instantaneously. LRT's treatment of nonlocality (Paper VI) distributes nonseparability to $I_\infty$ and locality to $A_\Omega$, avoiding primitive nonlocal dynamics.

## 7. Objections and Responses

### 7.1 "This Just Relabels the Problem"

**Objection:** You have replaced "collapse" with "logical resolution" and "$A$ delivers it." The explanatory situation is unchanged: we still don't know why one outcome rather than another occurs.

**Response:** The objection conflates two questions. *Why does a definite outcome occur?* — this is answered: $L_3$ requires it once decoherence provides the conditions. *Why this outcome rather than that one?* — this is answered probabilistically: the Born rule gives the probability of each outcome, and $A$ is the primitive that selects one. The demand for a deeper answer to the second question is the demand for a deterministic explanation of an irreducibly probabilistic process. No interpretation provides this. The Resolution Thesis does not claim to explain why a particular outcome occurs; it claims to explain why a *definite* outcome must occur and why asking for a dynamical mechanism behind that definiteness is a category error.

### 7.2 "Decoherence Is Not Sharp Enough"

**Objection:** Decoherence suppresses off-diagonal terms but never exactly eliminates them. The density matrix is never exactly diagonal. So the alternatives are never *exactly* mutually exclusive, and $L_3$ never *exactly* applies.

**Response:** This objection is addressed directly by the Zero-Gap identity (§3). The actualization threshold is not a foundational gap but a calculational task within standard quantum mechanics. The relevant variables — coupling Hamiltonian, environmental spectral density, system Hilbert-space dimension — are already identified in the decoherence literature (§3.2).

Two further points sharpen the response. First, decoherence is not merely approximate in a worrying sense. For macroscopic systems, the off-diagonal suppression factor is of order $e^{-N}$ where $N$ is the number of environmental degrees of freedom — a number so astronomically small (for pointer-scale objects, typically $10^{-10^{20}}$ or smaller) that no physical process could ever detect the residual coherence. Principled Physical Completeness (§3.4) establishes that resolution is complete when coherence is operationally suppressed, not when it is mathematically zero.

Second, the threshold question is not unique to LRT. Every interpretation that appeals to decoherence faces the same issue. The Many-Worlds program must specify when branching occurs (the "branch structure problem"). The decoherence-alone program must specify when the mixture becomes "classical enough." GRW/CSL parameterize the threshold with free parameters. LRT requires no free parameters — the threshold is determined by the physics of the specific interaction (§3.3).

### 7.3 "The Primitives Are Doing No Work — Decoherence Already Explains Everything"

**Objection:** Decoherence explains the preferred basis, the timescales, and the emergence of classicality. Adding $L_3$ and $A$ is unnecessary metaphysical baggage.

**Response:** Decoherence does not explain why a definite outcome occurs (§6.2). This is not a controversial claim; it is acknowledged by the leading proponents of the decoherence program. Zurek (2003) explicitly notes that decoherence explains the selection of the pointer basis and the suppression of interference but does not by itself yield definite outcomes. Schlosshauer's (2005, 2007) comprehensive reviews reach the same conclusion.

$L_3$ and $A$ are not baggage; they are the missing components that complete the account. Without them, the decoherence program either (a) silently appeals to something else (the Born rule postulate, an observer, an Everettian branching structure) or (b) claims a solution it has not provided.

### 7.4 "$A$ Is Just God-of-the-Gaps for Quantum Mechanics"

**Objection:** Invoking an irreducible "actualization primitive" is no different from saying "and then a miracle happens."

**Response:** $A$ is not invoked to fill a gap in our knowledge. It is posited as a primitive — a terminus of explanation, like charge in electrodynamics or spacetime in general relativity. Every physical theory has primitives that are not further explained. The question is whether the primitives are well-motivated and whether they do explanatory work.

$A$ is motivated by the transcendental argument (Paper I): the denial of actualization is self-undermining, since denying that anything is actual is itself an actual claim. $A$ does explanatory work: together with $L_3$, it forces PVM structure, the Born rule, and definite outcomes. A primitive that generates this much structure from this little input is not a gap-filler; it is an economical foundation.

## 8. Falsifiability and Empirical Predictions

### 8.1 The Null Prediction

LRT makes a prediction that is unusual in the foundations of physics: a universal negative. The theory predicts that no spontaneous collapse event occurs at any scale, for any system, at any rate. The GRW collapse rate $\lambda$ does not exist as a physical parameter. The CSL localization width $r_C$ does not exist. The entire parameter space of dynamical collapse models is empty.

This is not a gap in the theory or a failure to engage with experiment. It is a direct consequence of the Resolution Thesis. If measurement outcomes are logical resolutions under $L_3$ rather than dynamical interruptions of unitary evolution, then there is no stochastic collapse process to parameterize. The Schrödinger equation is not modified. No noise field couples to the wavefunction. No spontaneous localization occurs. Every apparent "collapse" is decoherence-driven resolution, calculable from the system-environment interaction Hamiltonian without supplementary parameters.

The prediction is unusually crisp for the foundations of physics, where interpretive questions are often regarded as empirically inert. LRT says: $\lambda = 0$, $r_C$ is absent, and any confirmed detection of a GRW/CSL-type anomaly (anomalous heating, spontaneous radiation from charged particles, deviations from unitarity in matter-wave interferometry) would falsify the Resolution Thesis.

### 8.2 Experimental Status

Current experiments constrain the GRW/CSL parameter space from multiple directions:

- **Matter-wave interferometry** with large molecules (Fein *et al.* 2019) confirms unitarity at mass scales where some collapse models predict deviations.
- **Spontaneous radiation searches** (Donadi *et al.* 2021) bound the CSL noise field through X-ray emission data, excluding Adler's enhanced collapse rate.
- **Optomechanical experiments** (Vinante *et al.* 2020) constrain collapse-induced heating in mechanical oscillators.
- **Gravitational wave detectors** (Carlesso *et al.* 2022) provide independent bounds on the CSL noise spectrum.

No experiment has detected a spontaneous collapse event. The parameter space available to GRW/CSL has been progressively narrowed. The original GRW values ($\lambda \approx 10^{-16}$ s$^{-1}$, $r_C \approx 10^{-7}$ m) remain within the allowed region but under increasing experimental pressure. Adler's enhanced rate ($\lambda \approx 10^{-8\pm2}$ s$^{-1}$) is already excluded by several independent experiments (Donadi *et al.* 2021; Vinante *et al.* 2020).

LRT's prediction ($\lambda = 0$, $r_C$ nonexistent) is currently winning.

### 8.3 The Uniqueness Claim

LRT occupies a distinctive position among realist interpretations of quantum mechanics. It is the only realist interpretation that simultaneously:

1. **Answers "why these axioms?"** The quantum formalism is derived from $\chi \equiv [L_3 : I_\infty : A]$ via the 13-step reconstruction (Paper II). The Hilbert space structure, Born rule, and unitary dynamics are not postulated but obtained.

2. **Makes a novel empirical prediction.** The null prediction ($\lambda = 0$, no spontaneous collapse at any scale) is not shared by the standard formalism, which is silent on whether collapse is dynamical. It is a prediction of the interpretation, not of the formalism it interprets.

3. **Is currently favored by experiment.** Every null result in collapse searches is consistent with LRT and inconsistent with portions of the GRW/CSL parameter space.

No other realist interpretation combines all three. Everettian quantum mechanics makes no novel empirical predictions beyond the standard formalism; it is, by design, empirically inert. No experiment can favor or disfavor it over any other interpretation, because all outcomes occur in some branch regardless of experimental result. Bohmian mechanics reproduces the quantum predictions exactly, adding no new testable content. GRW/CSL makes novel predictions but does not derive the quantum formalism from deeper principles. Copenhagen and QBism are not realist.

The methodological gap between LRT and Everett is worth stating directly. Many-Worlds achieves immunity to falsification as a feature, not a bug: by positing that all outcomes are real, it cannot be wrong about which outcome occurs. But this immunity comes at the cost of scientific utility. A theory that no experiment can touch is not on that account false, but it is explanatorily inferior to one that takes on empirical risk and survives. LRT takes on empirical risk — any confirmed detection of a GRW-type anomaly would falsify it — and experiment has so far returned consistently in its favor.

## 9. The Resolution Thesis and the Unity of LRT

### 9.1 Connections to Prior Papers

The Resolution Thesis is not a standalone proposal. It is the natural terminus of the LRT program as developed across Papers 0-VI:

- **Paper 0 (TRM Foundations):** Established the co-primitive ontology $\chi \equiv [L_3 : I_\infty : A]$ and the bridge equation $A_\Omega = L_3(I_\infty)$.
- **Paper I (Transcendental Argument):** Defended the transcendental necessity of $L_3$ and $A$, and the pragmatic necessity of $I_\infty$.
- **Paper II (Core Physics):** Derived the 13-step reconstruction chain from $\chi$ to the Schrödinger equation, establishing PVM structure (Step 4-5), the Born rule (Step 6), and unitary dynamics (Steps 7-10).
- **Paper IV (How Come the Quantum):** Showed that quantum structure is a downstream consequence of $\chi$, answering Wheeler's question.
- **Paper V (Measurement):** Developed the partial actualization framework, identifying the $I_\infty \to A_\Omega$ interface as the locus of measurement and the actualization threshold as the principal open problem.
- **Paper VI (Entanglement):** Demonstrated that nonlocality resides in $I_\infty$ (non-decomposable configurations) while locality resides in $A_\Omega$ (Boolean actualization is local).

The Resolution Thesis synthesizes these results. The "measurement problem" dissolves because:

1. The formalism is grounded, not brute (Paper IV).
2. The two-domain ontology ($I_\infty$ and $A_\Omega$) is motivated (Papers 0, I, II).
3. The interface between domains is characterized by partial actualization (Paper V).
4. Nonlocal correlations are consistent with local actualization (Paper VI).
5. The demand for a collapse mechanism is a category error — confusing a logical requirement for a dynamical deficit.

### 9.2 What Remains Open

The Resolution Thesis does not close all questions. Two principal open problems remain:

**The actualization threshold in specific regimes.** The Zero-Gap identity (§3) establishes that the actualization threshold is determined by interaction variables calculable within standard quantum mechanics, not by free parameters or foundational stipulations. What remains is the systematic characterization of threshold conditions across specific physical regimes — particularly mesoscopic systems where decoherence timescales are long enough to be experimentally probed, and quantum-gravitational regimes where the interaction Hamiltonian is not well-defined. The formal characterization in terms of the decoherence factor is clear in principle; the open work is its application to boundary cases.

**The relativistic extension.** The Resolution Thesis is developed within non-relativistic quantum mechanics. The extension to quantum field theory — where "measurement" is even less well-defined and the notion of a preferred time-slice is unavailable — remains open. Paper II (§8) identified this as the principal limitation of the current reconstruction.

## 10. Conclusion

The word "collapse" has haunted quantum mechanics for a century. It has generated an industry of interpretations, modifications, and philosophical perplexities — all because of an unexamined presupposition: that the wavefunction is ontologically primary and that any transition from superposition to definite outcome requires a dynamical account.

The Resolution Thesis rejects this presupposition. Under LRT, the wavefunction is a description of a configuration in $I_\infty$. Definite outcomes are configurations in $A_\Omega$. The transition is not a dynamical event but the completion of logical resolution: decoherence provides the physical conditions, $L_3$ provides the logical verdict, $A$ delivers the outcome.

The measurement problem was not unsolvable. It was malformed. It asked for a physical mechanism behind a logical requirement. Once the question is corrected, the answer is already present in the primitive ontology that LRT has been developing since Paper 0.

Quantum "collapse" is a category error. The correct term is *resolution*.
