## Abstract

Why is there determinate being rather than indeterminacy? Standard answers either terminate in brute fact or generate infinite regress. This paper presents the Transcendental Argument for Being (TAB), which derives determinate existence from a primitive that cannot coherently be denied. We define X ≡ [L₃ : I∞ : A]: the mutual constitution of logic, information, and action. Each component is transcendentally necessary: denying it presupposes it. From X, we derive the Core Equation A_Ω = L₃(I∞), which states that the actualized domain is identical to the logical filtration of all possible configurations. We then show that, given X and standard regularity assumptions (continuity, minimality, composition, non-contextual probability), quantum mechanical formalism (projective Hilbert space, Born rule, Schrödinger equation) is the unique mathematical structure satisfying these constraints. The derivation is deductive at each stage: given X plus the regularity assumptions, determinate being and its quantum structure follow necessarily. Logic Realism Theory (LRT) thus grounds physics in metaphysics rather than the reverse.

## 1. Introduction

The question "Why is there something rather than nothing?" has occupied philosophers since Leibniz. But there is a prior question, often overlooked: Why is there *determinate* being rather than indeterminacy? Even granting that something exists, we must explain why it has definite properties, why states are distinguishable, why the world admits of structure at all.

Standard approaches fail to answer this question satisfactorily:

**Brute fact:** Some philosophers hold that existence is simply given, requiring no further explanation. But this terminates inquiry rather than resolving it. Brute fact is the admission that we have no answer.

**Cosmological regress:** Classical arguments trace contingent beings back to a necessary being. But these arguments establish *that* a necessary ground exists, not *why* that ground produces determinate structure rather than chaos or indeterminacy.

**Modal realism:** David Lewis proposed that all possible worlds exist equally. This multiplies entities without explaining why any world is determinate rather than a blur of unrealized possibilities.

We propose a different approach. The Transcendental Argument for Being (TAB) does not argue from the concept of being to its existence (that would be ontological), nor from contingent existence to a necessary cause (that would be cosmological). TAB asks: given that there *is* being, what must be true about its logical structure? The answer, we argue, is that being must emerge from a primitive whose components cannot coherently be denied.

**Thesis:** Determinate being follows necessarily from the primitive X ≡ [L₃ : I∞ : A], where L₃ denotes the three fundamental laws of logic, I∞ denotes infinite information space (pure indeterminacy), and A denotes primitive action (the capacity for state transition). The interaction of these co-constitutive elements yields the Core Equation A_Ω = L₃(I∞). Given X and standard regularity assumptions (continuity, minimality, composition, non-contextual probability), quantum mechanical structure follows as the unique satisfying formalism.

**Paper structure:** Section 2 states axioms and definitions. Section 3 derives the Core Equation from the primitives. Section 4 derives quantum mechanical structure from the Core Equation. Section 5 addresses objections. Section 6 concludes.

## 2. Axioms and Definitions

We begin by stating our primitives explicitly. The argument that follows is deductive: each step follows necessarily from what precedes it. The primitives themselves are not arbitrary postulates but transcendental necessities, as we shall demonstrate.

### Definition 1: L₃ (The Three Laws of Logic)

L₃ comprises the three fundamental laws of classical logic:

- **Law of Identity (LOI):** A = A. A thing is what it is.
- **Law of Non-Contradiction (LNC):** ¬(A ∧ ¬A). A thing cannot both be and not be in the same respect at the same time.
- **Law of Excluded Middle (LEM):** A ∨ ¬A. For any proposition, either it or its negation holds.

These laws are not conventions, not linguistic rules, not psychological tendencies. They are conditions for the possibility of coherent thought and determinate existence. Aristotle recognized this in *Metaphysics* Book IV: LNC is the most certain of all principles because anyone who denies it must presuppose it in the act of denial (Aristotle, *Metaphysics* 1005b19-20).

### Definition 2: I∞ (Infinite Information Space)

I∞ denotes the totality of all representable configurations: every possible state, every possible arrangement, every possible distinction. It is pure indeterminacy in the sense that no configuration is privileged over any other. I∞ contains all possibilities undifferentiated.

Information here is understood in the sense of Wheeler's "It from Bit" (Wheeler, 1989): the fundamental currency of reality, more primitive than matter or energy. Distinctions, configurations, and states are informational before they are physical.

### Definition 3: A (Primitive Action)

A denotes the minimal capacity for state transition: the ontic ground of change itself. A operates as binary instantiation, distinguishing actual from non-actual. Without A, nothing happens. Constraint alone (L₃) can forbid certain configurations, but forbidding is not resolving. Resolution requires action.

A is not an external force applied to a pre-existing substance. It is constitutive of the possibility of change. Deny primitive action and nothing occurs, including the denial.

### Axiom 1: Mutual Constitution (X ≡ [L₃ : I∞ : A])

The colon notation marks mutual constitution, not conjunction. L₃, I∞, and A are not three separate entities that happen to coexist. Each is what it is only in relation to the others:

- L₃ without I∞ has nothing to constrain.
- I∞ without L₃ has no structure.
- Either without A has no actualization.

X names this tri-constitutive primitive. It is not a thing but the condition for the possibility of things.

### Axiom 2: Transcendental Necessity

Each component of X is transcendentally necessary: denying it presupposes it.

**L₃ is transcendentally necessary:** To deny L₃, one must make an assertion. An assertion has determinate content that excludes its negation. The denial of LNC ("contradictions can be true") is itself a determinate claim that excludes its negation. The denial presupposes what it denies. L₃ cannot be coherently rejected.

**I∞ is transcendentally necessary:** To deny that reality involves distinguishable states is to make a distinction (between realities with distinguishable states and realities without them). The claim "there are no distinctions" is itself a distinction. The denial presupposes what it denies. Information cannot be coherently rejected.

**A is transcendentally necessary:** To deny primitive action, one must perform an action (the denial). Thought, speech, and change of any kind presuppose the capacity for state transition. The denial presupposes what it denies. Action cannot be coherently rejected.

Since each component is transcendentally necessary, and since they are mutually constitutive, X itself is transcendentally necessary. X is not a hypothesis we ask you to grant. It is the condition for the possibility of coherent denial. You cannot reject X without presupposing X.

### 2.4 Regularity Assumptions

The derivation from X to quantum structure invokes additional assumptions beyond the primitives themselves. We state them explicitly here:

**R1. Continuity of A:** Action operates continuously rather than discretely. State transitions admit infinitesimal change. This assumption grounds unitary evolution and the differential structure of the Schrödinger equation.

**R2. Minimality/Parsimony:** Among structures satisfying the logical constraints, we select the minimal one—the structure with no extraneous features beyond what the constraints require. This grounds the preference for complex Hilbert space over quaternionic alternatives.

**R3. Standard Composition:** Composite systems combine according to tensor product structure. The state space of a system composed of subsystems A and B is H_A ⊗ H_B. This grounds entanglement and the non-separability of quantum states.

**R4. Non-contextual Probability:** Probability assignments to outcomes depend only on the state and the measurement, not on which other compatible measurements might be performed alongside. This grounds Gleason's theorem and the Born rule derivation.

These assumptions are not arbitrary. Each can be motivated as the natural or default reading of the primitives:

- **R1** follows from the absence of any privileged discrete scale in X.
- **R2** is the Occamist default: do not postulate structure beyond what constraints demand.
- **R3** follows from the informational nature of I∞: composite configurations are products of component configurations.
- **R4** follows from the objectivity of A_Ω: what is actual does not depend on what else might be measured.

Nevertheless, we mark them explicitly. The main argument is: **X plus R1–R4 yields quantum mechanics as the unique satisfying structure.** This is a strong result. The regularity assumptions are standard in reconstruction programs (Hardy, Chiribella et al.) but typically taken as primitive. Our contribution is grounding X transcendentally and motivating R1–R4 as natural consequences rather than brute postulates.

## 3. Derivation Part I: From X to A_Ω (The Core Equation)

We now derive the Core Equation A_Ω = L₃(I∞) from the primitive X. The derivation proceeds in three steps, each following necessarily from the preceding.

### Step 1: I∞ under L₃ is self-defeating

Let χ denote a primitive ontic state: whatever exists prior to any determination. We cannot begin with nothing, for nothing has no resources from which anything could arise. We must therefore begin with *something*.

If χ is fully determinate from the outset, we face an infinite regress: what determined it? The only coherent starting point is I∞: pure indeterminacy, all possibilities undifferentiated.

But I∞ cannot persist as such. It violates each law of L₃:

**I∞ violates LOI:** I∞ has no determinate identity. It is not what it is, because it is not any particular thing. The question "What is I∞?" has no answer, because I∞ is every configuration and none.

**I∞ violates LNC:** I∞ embodies contradiction. For any property P, I∞ both has P (as one of its configurations) and lacks P (as another). I∞ is the conjunction of all contradictions.

**I∞ violates LEM:** I∞ refuses resolution. For any proposition about I∞, neither the proposition nor its negation determinately holds. I∞ is neither P nor not-P; it is both and neither.

L₃ is not a law imposed on I∞ from outside. L₃ is the condition for the possibility of coherent existence. I∞, as pure indeterminacy, is incoherent. It cannot exist as such.

### Step 2: Constraint requires action to become operative

L₃ forbids I∞. But forbidding is not resolving. L₃ alone is a set of constraints, not an agent of change. L₃ specifies what cannot be but does not by itself bring about what is.

This is where A becomes essential. A is the capacity for state transition, the minimal ground of change. Without A, L₃ would forbid I∞, but nothing would happen. The indeterminate would remain indeterminate, violating the very constraints that forbid it.

With A, the constraint becomes operative. A is not an external force but the constitutive ground of resolution. A is what it means for logical constraint to take effect.

### Step 3: A under L₃ yields the Core Equation

A operates on I∞ subject to L₃ constraints. The result is not arbitrary selection but logical filtration. A resolves indeterminacy not by choosing randomly but by actualizing precisely those configurations that satisfy L₃.

This yields the **Core Equation:**

$$A_\Omega = L_3(I_\infty)$$

The equation states that the actualized domain (A_Ω) is identical to the result of applying logical constraints (L₃) to all possible configurations (I∞). What exists is precisely what logic permits.

**Left side:** A_Ω, the actualized domain, what is.

**Right side:** L₃(I∞), logic applied to information, what must be.

The equation asserts their identity. The actualized domain is not a subset of logically permitted configurations, chosen by some additional principle. It *is* the logically permitted configurations, actualized by A.

### The Core Equation as Consequence, Not Stipulation

The Core Equation is not a hypothesis we propose. It follows necessarily from the primitives:

1. I∞ alone is indeterminate (violates L₃).
2. L₃ alone constrains but does not actualize.
3. A is the operation that produces actuality.
4. A operating under L₃ on I∞ produces exactly what satisfies L₃.
5. Therefore A_Ω = L₃(I∞) is not a postulate but a consequence.

The transition χ → A_Ω(χ) is logically necessary. Being is not a brute fact but the inevitable resolution of primitive indeterminacy through primitive action under logical constraint.

## 4. Derivation Part II: From A_Ω to Quantum Structure

The Core Equation establishes that the actualized domain is the logical filtration of information space. We now show that, given the regularity assumptions R1–R4 (Section 2.4), quantum mechanical formalism is the unique mathematical structure satisfying these constraints.

### Step 4: A_Ω induces projective Hilbert space

The structure of A_Ω is not arbitrary. The constraints that define it, together with the regularity assumptions, induce specific mathematical geometry.

**Why Hilbert space:** States in A_Ω must be distinguishable (by LOI), mutually exclusive where contradictory (by LNC), and exhaustive of possibilities (by LEM). A complex Hilbert space H provides the natural arena for such states: vectors represent configurations, orthogonality represents mutual exclusion, and completeness represents exhaustiveness.

**Why projective:** Physical states are rays in Hilbert space, not vectors. Two vectors differing by a phase factor represent the same state. This projectivization (CP(H) rather than H itself) emerges from the fact that what matters is the *configuration*, not arbitrary labeling conventions. Normalization to unit vectors ensures we are tracking states, not magnitudes.

**Why complex over real or quaternionic:** Real Hilbert spaces lack the phase structure needed for interference. Quaternionic spaces introduce unnecessary complexity without additional explanatory power. Complex Hilbert space is the Goldilocks structure: rich enough to encode the constraints, minimal enough to contain nothing extraneous. *[Invokes R2: minimality.]*

**Why tensor composition:** Composite systems combine via tensor product (H_A ⊗ H_B). This grounds entanglement as a structural feature of composite actuality. *[Invokes R3: standard composition.]*

**Superposition as unresolved actuality:** A superposition |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ represents a state where actuality is distributed across configurations. This is not indeterminacy in the sense of I∞ (which violates L₃) but structured indeterminacy that *respects* L₃. The coefficients α and β encode how actuality is weighted across logically compatible configurations.

### Step 5: Born rule from L₃ constraints

Why is probability given by |ψ|² and not some other measure?

**Probability as degree of actualization:** The Born rule states that the probability of outcome i is |⟨i|ψ⟩|². This squared modulus emerges from the requirement that probabilities be real, non-negative, and sum to unity (normalization).

Gleason's theorem (Gleason, 1957) establishes that in Hilbert spaces of dimension ≥ 3, the only probability measure satisfying reasonable constraints is the Born rule. *[Invokes R4: non-contextual probability.]* This mathematical result has deeper roots: L₃ demands that actualization be consistent (LNC) and exhaustive (LEM). The Born rule is the unique measure satisfying these logical requirements in the Hilbert space framework.

**Why |ψ|² and not |ψ| or |ψ|⁴:** The squared modulus preserves the inner product structure of Hilbert space, which encodes distinguishability (LOI). Other powers would distort this structure, violating the logical constraints that generate the space.

### Step 6: Schrödinger equation from continuity of A

*[This step invokes R1: continuity of A.]*

**Why unitary evolution:** A_Ω must be preserved under temporal evolution. If the actualized domain is L₃(I∞), then evolution cannot take states outside this domain. Unitary operators preserve the inner product and hence the logical structure of Hilbert space. Non-unitary evolution would violate the constraints that define A_Ω.

**Why linear:** Linearity ensures that superpositions evolve consistently. If |ψ₁⟩ evolves to U|ψ₁⟩ and |ψ₂⟩ evolves to U|ψ₂⟩, then α|ψ₁⟩ + β|ψ₂⟩ must evolve to αU|ψ₁⟩ + βU|ψ₂⟩. Non-linear evolution would make the actualization of superpositions inconsistent with the actualization of their components, violating LNC.

**Why first-order in time:** The Schrödinger equation iℏ∂|ψ⟩/∂t = H|ψ⟩ is first-order in time. This reflects the fact that A operates as binary instantiation (actual vs. non-actual) at each moment. Second-order equations would require initial conditions on both state and rate of change, introducing structure not demanded by the primitives.

### Conclusion: QM as Necessary Structure

Quantum mechanics is not contingent physics—not one possible set of physical laws among many that might have obtained. Given X and the regularity assumptions R1–R4, it is the unique mathematical structure satisfying the logical constraints that define A_Ω.

The derivation chain is complete:

$$X + (R1\text{–}R4) \rightarrow A_\Omega = L_3(I_\infty) \rightarrow CP(H) \rightarrow \text{Born rule} \rightarrow \text{Schrödinger equation}$$

The first arrow (X → A_Ω) is purely transcendental: it follows from the primitives alone. The subsequent arrows invoke the regularity assumptions, which are natural defaults but not themselves transcendentally necessary. This is still a strong result: physics is grounded in metaphysics, with the contingent element reduced to a small set of structural assumptions that are standard across reconstruction programs.

## 5. Objections and Replies

### Objection 1: Why should we accept the axioms?

**Reply:** We do not ask you to accept the axioms as postulates. We argue that they are transcendentally necessary: denying them presupposes them. The skeptic who rejects L₃ must use L₃ to formulate the rejection. The skeptic who denies information must make a distinction. The skeptic who denies action must act. These are not rhetorical moves but demonstrations of self-refutation.

If an objector claims the transcendental arguments fail, the burden is to show how one can coherently deny L₃, I∞, or A without presupposing them. Until that is done, the axioms stand.

### Objection 2: Is the derivation really deductive?

**Reply:** We distinguish two phases:

*Phase I (X → A_Ω):* This derivation proceeds by necessary entailment from the primitives alone:

- I∞ violates L₃ (definitional consequence of I∞ and L₃).
- Constraint without action cannot resolve (conceptual truth about constraint and resolution).
- A under L₃ on I∞ yields L₃(I∞) (direct application of terms).

*Phase II (A_Ω → QM):* This derivation invokes the regularity assumptions R1–R4 (Section 2.4). Given A_Ω plus these assumptions, the steps are deductive. But the assumptions themselves are not transcendentally necessary—they are natural defaults, motivated by the structure of X and standard across reconstruction programs.

This is not a weakness but an honest scope claim. The result remains strong: we have reduced the contingent element in physics to four simple structural assumptions, while grounding the primitive (X) transcendentally. Compare this to standard physics, which takes the entire quantum formalism as brute fact.

If the objector claims there is a gap within either phase, the burden is to identify it.

### Objection 3: Why this Hilbert space?

**Reply:** The question is why physical states inhabit a complex Hilbert space rather than some other mathematical structure (real vector space, quaternionic space, etc.).

The answer involves both L₃ and the minimality assumption (R2). The logical constraints from L₃ require:

- Distinguishability via inner product (LOI)
- Orthogonality for mutual exclusion (LNC)
- Completeness for exhaustiveness (LEM)

The regularity assumptions add:

- Superposition (unresolved actuality across configurations)
- Continuous evolution (R1: continuity of A)
- Tensor composition (R3: standard composition)
- Minimality (R2: no extraneous structure)

Real Hilbert spaces lack the phase structure needed for interference. Quaternionic spaces introduce unnecessary complexity without additional explanatory power. Complex Hilbert space is the Goldilocks structure: rich enough to encode the constraints, minimal enough to contain nothing extraneous.

Hardy (2001) and Chiribella et al. (2011) have shown from different starting points that reasonable axioms uniquely determine quantum theory over alternatives. Our contribution is twofold: (1) grounding X transcendentally, and (2) motivating R1–R4 as natural consequences of X rather than brute postulates. This reduces the unexplained element in physics to structural defaults rather than fundamental laws.

### Objection 4: What about alternative logics?

**Reply:** Paraconsistent and intuitionistic logics are sometimes offered as alternatives to classical logic, suggesting that L₃ is not uniquely necessary.

**The metalanguage reply:** Paraconsistent systems (Priest, 2006) tolerate contradictions without explosion, and intuitionistic logic (Dummett, 1977) rejects LEM, holding that a proposition is true only if constructively provable. But both presuppose classical logic in their metalanguage. The claim "paraconsistent logic is a coherent alternative" is itself a classical assertion with determinate content excluding its negation. The intuitionistic rejection of LEM presupposes that either LEM holds or it doesn't. Alternative logics are technical variations developed within the framework of L₃. They do not escape it.

This metalanguage reply is correct but incomplete. We can offer a stronger response.

**The ontological reply:** L₃ is not primarily a law of thought or a rule of inference. It is a constraint on *being*. Aristotle's formulation is explicitly ontological: "the same attribute cannot at the same time belong and not belong to the same subject in the same respect" (*Metaphysics* 1005b19-20). This is not a claim about what we can say or think. It is a claim about what can *exist*.

The critical distinction is between **semantic expressibility** and **physical actualizability**. We can coherently *express* paradoxes. The liar sentence ("This sentence is false") is grammatical, has semantic content, and generates interesting puzzles about truth-value assignment. Russell's paradox, sorites, the Grelling-Nelson paradox—all are semantically expressible and logically engaging.

But none of them describe physical states of affairs.

The liar paradox is about sentences and self-reference, not configurations of matter, energy, or information. "This sentence is false" has no physical instantiation because there is no physical system whose state satisfies that description. The paradox exists in the semantic register—it concerns truth-value assignments to linguistic objects—not the ontological register of what can be actualized.

**Dialetheism as semantic theory:** Priest's dialetheism holds that some contradictions are true. But "true" here operates in the semantic sense: certain sentences receive the truth value "true" even when they also receive "false." This is a theory about truth-value assignments to propositions. It does not entail, and cannot show, that physical reality instantiates contradictions—that some physical system is simultaneously in state S and state ¬S in the same respect at the same time.

One can construct a formal semantics where A ∧ ¬A receives a designated value. One cannot instantiate a round square.

**Physical actualization requires determination:** To *be* at all is to be *this* and not *that*. Determination is constitutive of existence. An entity that both has property P and lacks property P in the same respect at the same time is not a "strange entity"—it is no entity at all. There is no *thing* that satisfies the description, not because we lack imagination, but because being requires identity.

This is why I∞ cannot exist *as such*. Pure indeterminacy has no determinate identity. It is every configuration and none. It cannot be a state of affairs because states of affairs require determination. I∞ is not an exotic entity but an impossibility that our notation allows us to reference.

A_Ω, by contrast, is precisely what *can* be. A_Ω = L₃(I∞) is the set of configurations that satisfy the constraints necessary for existence. Action (A) resolves I∞ not arbitrarily but by actualizing what logic permits. The result is determinate being: states that have identity, exclude their negations, and exhaust the space of possibilities.

**The dialetheist cannot escape this.** If Priest responds that some physical contradictions *are* instantiated—say, in quantum superposition—we reply that superposition is not contradiction. The state |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ does not mean "the system is 0 and not-0 in the same respect." It means the system is in a third state, |ψ⟩, which is *neither* |0⟩ *nor* |1⟩ but has weighted relations to both. Superposition respects L₃; it distributes actuality across compatible configurations within the constraints.

If the dialetheist insists that reality contains *genuine* contradictions beyond semantic paradoxes, the burden is to exhibit one—not a puzzle about sentences, but a physical system that both has and lacks a property in the same respect at the same time. No such exhibition has been made. The examples offered (liar, Russell, borderline cases) are invariably semantic or logical, never physical.

**This is falsifiable.** Logic Realism Theory does not assert L₃ by stipulation but as an empirical generalization about physical actualization that has never been falsified. If physical contradictions were possible, we should be able to:

1. **Exhibit a physical system** in contradictory states simultaneously—not superposition, but genuine A ∧ ¬A in the same respect.
2. **Measure contradictory outcomes** from a single preparation—not probabilistic distribution over exclusive outcomes, but determinate registration of "both P and ¬P."
3. **Construct technology** that exploits instantiated contradiction—not technology exploiting superposition (which respects L₃), but devices that function by actualizing impossibilities.

None of these have been demonstrated. Every apparent candidate resolves under scrutiny into:
- A semantic artifact (the sentence is problematic, not reality)
- A distributional state (probabilities over exclusive outcomes that obtain one at a time)
- A case of ambiguity (different respects, different times, or equivocation on terms)

The challenge is direct: show one physical contradiction. Not a paradox about sentences. Not a superposition that collapses to definite outcomes upon measurement. A genuine instantiated A ∧ ¬A.

Until that exhibition is produced, L₃ stands as unfalsified—not because we have defined it into necessity, but because every physical test confirms it. The ontological claim is thus both transcendentally motivated (denial presupposes it) and empirically supported (no counterexample in the physical domain). Dialetheism operates freely in the semantic register; it has no purchase on physical actualization.

Alternative logics are valuable tools for exploring consequence relations under non-classical assumptions. But they do not establish that reality can instantiate what they model. One can explore the logic of impossible worlds without asserting that impossible worlds exist. Logic Realism Theory takes L₃ as the constraint on *being*, not merely on *reasoning*. The ontological claim stands: what violates L₃ cannot be actualized, whatever interesting sentences we can construct about it.

## 6. Conclusion

We have presented the Transcendental Argument for Being (TAB), deriving determinate existence and its quantum structure from a primitive that cannot coherently be denied, supplemented by standard regularity assumptions.

**The derivation chain:**

1. X ≡ [L₃ : I∞ : A] is transcendentally necessary.
2. I∞ under L₃ is self-defeating, requiring resolution through A.
3. A under L₃ yields A_Ω = L₃(I∞), the Core Equation. *(Steps 1–3: from X alone.)*
4. A_Ω induces projective Hilbert space as its natural arena. *(Invokes R2, R3.)*
5. The Born rule follows from L₃ constraints on probability. *(Invokes R4.)*
6. The Schrödinger equation follows from continuity of A. *(Invokes R1.)*

TAB answers "why is there determinate being?" without appeal to brute fact, infinite regress, or multiplication of entities. Being is the necessary resolution of indeterminacy through action under logical constraint. The regularity assumptions (R1–R4) that carry us from A_Ω to quantum formalism are not brute postulates but natural defaults, motivated by the structure of X and standard across the reconstruction literature.

**Logic Realism Theory** is the research program that develops TAB's implications. Physics is grounded in metaphysics. Quantum mechanics is not contingent but necessary. The laws of physics are ultimately logical laws.

**Open problems remain:** How does general relativity emerge from these constraints? What determines the specific particle content of our universe? Why these coupling constants? These questions define the frontier of LRT research. But the foundation is established: reality is logical, informational, and dynamic because it cannot coherently be otherwise.
