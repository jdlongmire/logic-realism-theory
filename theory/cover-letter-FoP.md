## Summary

This paper establishes the minimal ontological structure required for determinate existence. I argue that three primitives—prescriptive logical constraint (L₃), infinite informational possibility (I∞), and actualization (A)—are each transcendentally necessary and jointly sufficient for the constitution of reality. Their interaction yields a core result: actuality coincides with the logically admissible informational configurations of the total possibility space, expressed as A_Ω = L₃(I∞).

## Contribution

The paper makes three contributions relevant to foundations of physics:

1. **Transcendental grounding for operational axioms.** Quantum reconstruction programs (Hardy 2001; Chiribella et al. 2011) derive quantum formalism from informational principles but leave those principles as unexplained postulates. This paper provides the ontological grounding beneath such axioms, explaining why physical states must satisfy the structural constraints that reconstruction programs assume.

2. **Explicit engagement with dialetheism and paraconsistent approaches.** The paper argues that paraconsistent quantum mechanics (da Costa & de Ronde 2013) conflates epistemic indeterminacy with ontological contradiction, and that this conflation is ruled out by the identity conditions required for determinate existence.

3. **Connection to formal verification.** The ontological framework has been partially formalized in Lean 4, with the physics reconstruction chain (from primitives to the Schrödinger equation) verified with zero errors. The formalization is documented in Appendix C.

## Fit for Foundations of Physics

The manuscript engages directly with the Wheeler "It from Bit" tradition, Tegmark's Mathematical Universe Hypothesis, and Floridi's informational structural realism. It addresses the question of why reality has the structure it does—a question at the heart of foundations of physics. The paper develops no new physical formalisms in this installment; it secures the ontological framework from which a companion paper (Part II) reconstructs quantum mechanics.
