# Information ontology and nonlocal logical coherence
Status: WORKING HYPOTHESIS / NOT DERIVED

## Author's emerging positions
1. **Information is ordered substance**, physical or nonphysical. The carrier underdetermines informational identity.
2. Logic and mathematics are nonphysical. Whether they qualify as *substance* under this ontology remains to be defined.
3. Logic determines admissibility of informational configurations; it does not necessarily select one actual configuration.
4. Information is considered in conceptual and coherent categories. These may overlap: a coherent conceptual representation is possible, while a coherent representation can describe an incoherent referent.
5. Physically actualizable information is a subset of coherent information; physically actual information is a subset of physically actualizable information.
6. No physically actualized configuration violates the three classical fundamental laws (identity, noncontradiction, excluded middle). This is an ontological axiom, not an experimentally established theorem.
7. **The universe may not be locally real, but it is logically real.**
8. **Logical information coherence transcends physical limitations**: logical consistency is independent of spatial distance. This does not establish unrestricted physical realization, signaling, or causal influence.

## Working distinctions
- Substance: ontological bearer of order; nonphysical substance requires a noncircular account.
- Carrier: particular physical or nonphysical realization/representation; underdetermines informational identity.
- Order: determinate structure/relations; should not be identified uncritically with low entropy, regularity, or Shannon information.
- Conceptual information: representational structure, including representations whose intended referents are impossible.
- Coherent information: structure satisfying the chosen logical admissibility criteria.
- Physical actualizability: a stronger predicate than logical coherence.
- Actuality: realized physical configuration.
